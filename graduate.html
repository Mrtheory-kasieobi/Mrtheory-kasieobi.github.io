Transcendental Number Theory Overview

An overview of transcendental number theory proceeds through several natural stages. We begin with elementary ideas: the distinction between algebraic and transcendental numbers and why transcendence is a fundamental question in number theory. The classical section introduces major results such as the Gelfond–Schneider and Lindemann–Weierstrass theorems, showing how these resolve deep questions about special constants. We then move to algebraic independence, which extends the basic notion of transcendence and leads to deeper results about multiple numbers. Next, we examine methods used in transcendence theory, including Diophantine approximation and zero estimates, to understand how such theorems are proved. Finally, we discuss modern questions, open problems, and directions for further study.
