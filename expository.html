Expository writing is one of the most important forms of mathematical communication. Its purpose is not to present new research, but rather to explain existing mathematics in a clear, logical, and accessible way. A well-written exposition bridges the gap between technical rigor and intuition, helping readers understand not only what is true, but also why it is true and how different ideas are connected. This page is my collection of expository writing, organized into two sections: Olympiad Mathematics contains selected contest solutions and mathematical commentary, while Mathematical Expositions gathers longer writing across algebra, analysis, number theory, geometry, and related areas. Some pieces introduce foundational concepts, while others provide detailed solutions, historical context, or connections to more advanced mathematics.
