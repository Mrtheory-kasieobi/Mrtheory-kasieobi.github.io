Calculus I Final Exam

Calculus I · 30 marks + 5 bonus

This exam contains 20 questions: Part A (10 multiple choice, 1 mark each) and Part B (10 written response, 2 marks each), followed by Part C (one bonus problem worth 5 marks).

Total: 30 graded marks plus a 5-mark bonus that is reported separately and does not change your percentage. Suggested time: 110 minutes. The problems range across the whole course, with an emphasis on ideas that connect derivatives and integrals. After submitting, you will see the correct choices, expected answer forms, and full worked solutions with alternative methods.

Final Exam - Calculus I

Part A: Multiple Choice (1 mark each)

Choose the best answer for each question. Each question has exactly one correct choice.

1.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{i^3}{n^4}\).

2.

Let \(\displaystyle g(x)=\int_{x^2}^{x^3}\sqrt{1+t^2}\,dt\). Find \(g'(x)\).

3.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^4}}\,dx\).

4.

Find the average value of \(f(x)=\sin x\) on \([0,\pi]\).

5.

Find the area of the region enclosed by \(y=x^2\) and \(y=2-x^2\).

6.

The region under \(y=\frac1x\) from \(x=1\) to \(x=2\) is rotated about the \(y\)-axis. Find the volume.

7.

Find \(\displaystyle\frac{d}{dx}\int_0^{x^2}\cos(t^3)\,dt\).

8.

A force of \(F(x)=x^2+1\) newtons acts over \(0 \le x \le 2\) metres. Find the work done.

9.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{x^2+4}\).

10.

If \(\int_0^2 f(x)\,dx=5\) and \(\int_0^3 f(x)\,dx=2\), find \(\int_2^3 f(x)\,dx\).

Part B: Written Response (2 marks each)

Show all of your work in the box provided. Exact answers are required.

11.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{\tan x-\sin x}{x^3}\). Use only the standard trigonometric limits.

12.

Identify the definite integral represented by the limit, then evaluate it: \(\displaystyle\lim_{n \to \infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{n}\sin\!\left(\frac{i\pi}{n}\right)\).

13.

Let \(\displaystyle y=\int_{\cos x}^{x^2} e^{t^2}\,dt\). Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\).

14.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int x\ln x\,dx\).

15.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int \frac{dx}{x^2-1}\) using partial fractions.

16.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos^2 x\,dx\) using a substitution with changed limits.

17.

The region between \(y=x\) and \(y=x^2\) for \(0 \le x \le 1\) is rotated about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume.

18.

Find the area of the region in the first quadrant bounded by \(y=x^2\) and \(x=y^2\). Use horizontal slices.

19.

A particle has velocity \(v(t)=t^2-4t+3\) m/s. Find the total distance travelled during \(0 \le t \le 4\).

20.

Find the number \(c \in [0,2]\) whose function value equals the average value of \(f(x)=x^2\) on \([0,2]\).

Part C: Bonus Problem (5 marks, reported separately)

Challenge problem. It is graded, but its marks are not added to the 30-mark total.

21 (bonus).

Let \(R_a\) be the region bounded by \(y=0\), \(y=\sqrt{x}\), and \(x=a\), where \(a \gt 0\). Find the unique \(a\) for which the area of \(R_a\) is numerically equal to the volume obtained by rotating \(R_a\) about the \(x\)-axis.

Worked Solutions

Each solution includes a standard method and an alternative route where one is natural. A different but valid approach earns full credit; the grader accepts common equivalent forms and key intermediate results.

  1. 1. \(\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{i^3}{n^4}=\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n\left(\frac{i}{n}\right)^3\to\int_0^1x^3\,dx=\frac14\). Alternative: use \(\sum i^3=\left(\frac{n(n+1)}{2}\right)^2\) and take the limit.
  2. 2. By the Leibniz rule, \(g'(x)=3x^2\sqrt{1+(x^3)^2}-2x\sqrt{1+(x^2)^2}=3x^2\sqrt{1+x^6}-2x\sqrt{1+x^4}\). Alternative: write \(g=F(x^3)-F(x^2)\) with \(F'(t)=\sqrt{1+t^2}\).
  3. 3. Let \(u=x^2\), \(du=2x\,dx\): \(\int\frac{x}{\sqrt{1-x^4}}dx=\frac12\int\frac{du}{\sqrt{1-u^2}}=\frac12\arcsin(x^2)+C\). Alternative: set \(x^2=\sin\theta\).
  4. 4. Average value \(=\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\sin x\,dx=\frac2\pi\). Alternative: the area under one hump of sine from \(0\) to \(\pi\) is \(2\).
  5. 5. Intersections satisfy \(x^2=2-x^2\), so \(x=\pm1\). Area \(=\int_{-1}^1(2-2x^2)\,dx=\frac83\). Alternative: by symmetry, \(2\int_0^1(2-2x^2)dx=\frac83\).
  6. 6. Shells: \(V=2\pi\int_1^2 x\cdot\frac1x\,dx=2\pi\). Alternative (Pappus): the area is \(\ln2\) and the centroid's \(x\)-coordinate is \(1/\ln2\), so \(V=2\pi(1)=2\pi\).
  7. 7. With \(F(u)=\int_0^u\cos(s^3)\,ds\), the derivative is \(F'(x^2)\cdot2x=2x\cos((x^2)^3)=2x\cos(x^6)\).
  8. 8. \(W=\int_0^2(x^2+1)\,dx=\left[\frac{x^3}{3}+x\right]_0^2=\frac83+2=\frac{14}{3}\) J.
  9. 9. \(\int\frac{dx}{x^2+4}=\frac12\arctan\frac{x}{2}+C\). Alternative: use \(\int\frac{dx}{x^2+a^2}=\frac1a\arctan\frac{x}{a}\) with \(a=2\).
  10. 10. \(\int_2^3f=\int_0^3f-\int_0^2f=2-5=-3\). The negative sign is possible because the interval is oriented left to right with a smaller accumulated integral at \(3\).
  11. 11. \(\tan x-\sin x=\sin x\left(\frac1{\cos x}-1\right)=\sin x\frac{1-\cos x}{\cos x}\), so the limit is \(1\cdot\frac12\cdot1=\frac12\). Alternative: \(\tan x=x+\frac{x^3}{3}+\cdots\) and \(\sin x=x-\frac{x^3}{6}+\cdots\), difference \(\frac{x^3}{2}\).
  12. 12. With \(\Delta x=\pi/n\), the sum is \(\frac1\pi\sum_{i=1}^n\sin(i\pi/n)\Delta x\to\frac1\pi\int_0^\pi\sin x\,dx=\frac2\pi\).
  13. 13. \(y'=e^{(x^2)^2}(2x)-e^{\cos^2x}(-\sin x)=2xe^{x^4}+\sin x\,e^{\cos^2x}\). Alternative: let \(F(t)=\int_0^t e^{s^2}ds\) and write \(y=F(x^2)-F(\cos x)\).
  14. 14. Integration by parts with \(u=\ln x\), \(dv=x\,dx\): \(\int x\ln x\,dx=\frac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\int\frac{x^2}{2}\cdot\frac1x\,dx=\frac{x^2}{2}\ln x-\frac{x^2}{4}+C\).
  15. 15. \(\frac1{x^2-1}=\frac12\left(\frac1{x-1}-\frac1{x+1}\right)\), so the integral is \(\frac12\ln\left|\frac{x-1}{x+1}\right|+C\). Alternative: keep the separated form \(\frac12\ln|x-1|-\frac12\ln|x+1|+C\).
  16. 16. Let \(u=\cos x\), \(du=-\sin x\,dx\); the limits \(x=0,\pi/2\) become \(u=1,0\). Thus \(\int_0^{\pi/2}\sin x\cos^2x\,dx=\int_0^1u^2\,du=\frac13\).
  17. 17. Washers: \(V=\pi\int_0^1\left(x^2-(x^2)^2\right)dx=\pi\left(\frac13-\frac15\right)=\frac{2\pi}{15}\). Alternative (shells): \(2\pi\int_0^1 y(\sqrt y-y)\,dy=\frac{2\pi}{15}\).
  18. 18. Horizontal slices run from \(x=y^2\) to \(x=\sqrt y\), so \(A=\int_0^1(\sqrt y-y^2)\,dy=\frac23-\frac13=\frac13\). Alternative (vertical slices): \(\int_0^1(\sqrt x-x^2)\,dx=\frac13\).
  19. 19. Zeros of \(v\) occur at \(t=1,3\). Each of \([0,1],[1,3],[3,4]\) contributes \(4/3\) to distance, so total distance \(=4\). Alternative: integrate \(|t^2-4t+3|\) piecewise.
  20. 20. Average value \(=\frac12\int_0^2x^2dx=\frac43\). Solve \(c^2=\frac43\) with \(c\in[0,2]\): \(c=\frac{2}{\sqrt3}=\frac{2\sqrt3}{3}\).
  21. 21 (bonus). Area \(A(a)=\int_0^a\sqrt x\,dx=\frac23a^{3/2}\). Volume \(V(a)=\pi\int_0^a(\sqrt x)^2dx=\frac{\pi a^2}{2}\). Setting \(A=V\) and dividing by \(a^{3/2}\gt0\) gives \(\frac23=\frac{\pi}{2}\sqrt a\), hence \(\sqrt a=\frac{4}{3\pi}\) and \(a=\frac{16}{9\pi^2}\). Alternative: divide first by \(a\) and solve \(\frac23\sqrt a=\frac{\pi}{2}a\).
