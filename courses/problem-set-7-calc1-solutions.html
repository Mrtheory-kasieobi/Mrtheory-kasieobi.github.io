Problem Set 7: Integration - SOLUTIONS

Calculus I - Week 7

Solution Guide

Note: These are detailed solutions showing all steps and reasoning. Each step is clearly justified.

Problem 1: \(\int (3x^2 - 6x + 5) \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Integrate term by term
\(\int 3x^2 dx - \int 6x dx + \int 5 dx\)
Step 2: Apply power rule
\(\int x^n dx = \frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}\)
Step 3: Compute each integral
\(\int 3x^2 dx = x^3\), \(\int 6x dx = 3x^2\), \(\int 5 dx = 5x\)
Step 4: Combine with constant
\(x^3 - 3x^2 + 5x + C\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{x^3 - 3x^2 + 5x + C}\)

Problem 2: \(\int_1^2 x^3 \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Find antiderivative
\(\int x^3 dx = \frac{x^4}{4}\)
Step 2: Apply FTC
\(\left[\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_1^2 = \frac{16}{4} - \frac{1}{4} = 4 - \frac{1}{4} = \frac{15}{4}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{15}{4}}\)

Problem 3: \(\int \frac{1}{x} \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Recall standard integral
\(\int \frac{1}{x} dx = \ln|x| + C\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\ln|x| + C}\)

Problem 4: \(\int_0^{\pi/4} \sin x \cos x \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Use substitution
Let \(u = \sin x\), then \(du = \cos x \, dx\)
Step 2: Change limits
When \(x = 0\): \(u = 0\). When \(x = \frac{\pi}{4}\): \(u = \sin\frac{\pi}{4} = \frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}\)
Step 3: Integrate
\(\int_0^{\sqrt{2}/2} u \, du = \frac{u^2}{2}\Big|_0^{\sqrt{2}/2} = \frac{(\sqrt{2}/2)^2}{2} = \frac{1/2}{2} = \frac{1}{4}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{1}{4}}\)

Problem 5: \(\int x e^x \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Set up integration by parts
\(u = x\), \(dv = e^x dx\)
Step 2: Find du and v
\(du = dx\), \(v = e^x\)
Step 3: Apply formula
\(\int u \, dv = uv - \int v \, du\)
Step 4: Compute
\(x e^x - \int e^x dx = x e^x - e^x + C\)
Step 5: Factor
\(e^x(x - 1) + C\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{e^x(x - 1) + C}\)

Problem 6: \(\int \frac{\ln x}{x} \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Use substitution
Let \(u = \ln x\), then \(du = \frac{1}{x}dx\)
Step 2: Rewrite integral
\(\int u \, du\)
Step 3: Integrate
\(\frac{u^2}{2} + C\)
Step 4: Substitute back
\(\frac{(\ln x)^2}{2} + C\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{(\ln x)^2}{2} + C}\)

Problem 7: \(\int_0^1 x(1-x) \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Expand integrand
\(x(1-x) = x - x^2\)
Step 2: Integrate term by term
\(\int_0^1 (x - x^2) dx = \left[\frac{x^2}{2} - \frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1\)
Step 3: Evaluate
\(\frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} = \frac{3-2}{6} = \frac{1}{6}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{1}{6}}\)

Problem 8: \(\int \frac{1}{x^2 + 4} \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Recognize arctangent form
\(\int \frac{dx}{x^2 + a^2} = \frac{1}{a}\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{a} + C\)
Step 2: Identify a
\(x^2 + 4 = x^2 + 2^2\), so \(a = 2\)
Step 3: Apply formula
\(\frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{2} + C\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\frac{x}{2} + C}\)

Problem 9: \(\int \sin^2 x \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Use half-angle identity
\(\sin^2 x = \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}\)
Step 2: Integrate
\(\int \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2} dx = \frac{1}{2}\int dx - \frac{1}{2}\int \cos 2x \, dx\)
Step 3: Compute
\(\frac{x}{2} - \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{\sin 2x}{2} = \frac{x}{2} - \frac{\sin 2x}{4}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{x}{2} - \frac{\sin 2x}{4} + C}\)

Problem 10: \(\int_0^{\pi/2} \sin^2 x \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Use half-angle identity
\(\sin^2 x = \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2}\)
Step 2: Integrate
\(\int_0^{\pi/2} \frac{1 - \cos 2x}{2} dx = \frac{1}{2}[x]_0^{\pi/2} - \frac{1}{4}[\sin 2x]_0^{\pi/2}\)
Step 3: Evaluate
\(\frac{\pi}{4} - 0 = \frac{\pi}{4}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{\pi}{4}}\)

Bonus 1: \(\int \frac{1}{x \ln x} \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Use substitution
Let \(u = \ln x\), \(du = \frac{1}{x}dx\)
Step 2: Rewrite
\(\int \frac{1}{u} du = \ln|u| + C\)
Step 3: Substitute back
\(\ln|\ln x| + C\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\ln|\ln x| + C}\)

Bonus 2: \(\int \sec x \tan x \, dx\)

Solution:
Step 1: Recognize standard form
\(\frac{d}{dx}[\sec x] = \sec x \tan x\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\sec x + C}\)
