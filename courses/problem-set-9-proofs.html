Problem Set 9: Divisibility and Modular Arithmetic

Introduction to Proofs - Lecture 9 Medium

Try each problem before reading the solutions. A proof is judged by the completeness of its logical chain, not by how short it is. State your assumptions, justify every nontrivial step, and verify that the conclusion follows exactly.

Problems

Problem 1

Prove that if \(a\equiv b\pmod n\) and \(c\equiv d\pmod n\), then \(ac\equiv bd\pmod n\).

Problem 2

Solve \(6x\equiv9\pmod{15}\) completely.

Problem 3

Prove that \(5 mid(n^2+2)\) for every integer \(n\).

Problem 4

Prove that every prime \(p>3\) satisfies \(p^2\equiv1\pmod{12}\).

Problem 5

Use the Chinese remainder theorem to solve \(x\equiv1\pmod4\), \(x\equiv2\pmod7\).

Problem 6

Prove that if \(p\) is prime and \(a^2\equiv1\pmod p\), then \(a\equiv1\) or \(a\equiv-1\pmod p\).

Problem 7

Compute \(7^{222}\) modulo \(13\) using Fermat's little theorem.

Problem 8

Prove that there are infinitely many primes congruent to \(3\pmod4\).

Bonus Problem

Prove that if \(x\) is odd, then \(x^2\equiv1\pmod8\).
