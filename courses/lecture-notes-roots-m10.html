Lecture 4: Roots and Powers

Mathematics 10 - Chapter 4

4.1 The Real Number System

The real number system includes rational and irrational numbers. Rational numbers can be expressed as fractions, while irrational numbers cannot.

Why This Classification Matters: Understanding the different types of numbers helps us know what operations are valid and what answers to expect. For example, the square root of a non-perfect square is irrational, which affects how we express answers.

Number Classification

Natural Numbers: 1, 2, 3, 4, ... (counting numbers)

Whole Numbers: 0, 1, 2, 3, ... (natural numbers plus zero)

Rational Numbers: Can be written as \(\frac{a}{b}\) where \(b \neq 0\)

Irrational Numbers: Cannot be written as fractions; decimal never ends or repeats

Real Numbers: All rational and irrational numbers

4.2 Simplifying Powers

Powers represent repeated multiplication. The rules for simplifying powers are essential for working with exponential expressions.

Connection to Real World: Powers appear in compound interest, population growth, radioactive decay, and computer memory. Understanding them is crucial for financial literacy and scientific applications.

Laws of Exponents

Product Rule: \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\)

Quotient Rule: \(\frac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}\)

Power Rule: \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)

Negative Exponent: \(a^{-n} = \frac{1}{a^n}\)

Zero Exponent: \(a^0 = 1\) (for \(a \neq 0\))

Example 1: Simplifying Powers

Simplify: \(\frac{(x^3 y^{-2})^4}{x^5 y^2}\)

\(= \frac{x^{12} y^{-8}}{x^5 y^2} = x^{12-5} y^{-8-2} = x^7 y^{-10} = \frac{x^7}{y^{10}}\)

Motivation: This skill is essential for simplifying algebraic expressions in calculus and physics.

4.3 Exponent Laws with Numeric Bases

When working with numbers, we can use calculators to verify our exponent laws. This helps build intuition for the rules.

Real-World Example: The Richter scale for earthquakes uses powers. An earthquake of magnitude 6 is 10 times more powerful than one of magnitude 5.

4.4 Math Lab: Exploring Powers

In this lab, you'll explore patterns in powers and verify the exponent laws using numerical examples.

Key Insight: The exponent laws work because of the fundamental definition of multiplication. When we multiply \(a^m \times a^n\), we're adding \(m\) factors of \(a\) to \(n\) factors of \(a\), giving \(m+n\) total factors.

4.5 Negative Exponents and Reciprocals

A negative exponent means we take the reciprocal of the base. This extends the exponent laws to all integers.

Why This Makes Sense: If \(a^3 \times a^{-3} = a^0 = 1\), then \(a^{-3}\) must equal \(\frac{1}{a^3}\). This maintains consistency in the exponent laws.

Example 2: Working with Negative Exponents

Simplify: \(\frac{3x^{-2}y^3}{2x^4y^{-1}}\)

\(= \frac{3y^3y^1}{2x^4x^2} = \frac{3y^4}{2x^6}\)

Motivation: Negative exponents appear in scientific notation and in formulas for things like electrical resistance and gravitational force.

4.6 Evaluating Powers with Rational Bases

When the base is a fraction, we apply the exponent to both numerator and denominator.

Application: In probability, we often work with fractions raised to powers. For example, the probability of getting 3 heads in a row is \((\frac{1}{2})^3 = \frac{1}{8}\).

4.7 Combining Laws of Exponents

Complex expressions may require multiple exponent laws. Work systematically from the innermost expressions outward.

Example 3: Complex Power Simplification

Simplify: \(\left(\frac{a^{-2}b^3}{a^4b^{-1}}\right)^{-2}\)

Step 1: Simplify inside parentheses: \(a^{-6}b^4\)

Step 2: Apply outer exponent: \(a^{12}b^{-8} = \frac{a^{12}}{b^8}\)

Motivation: This type of manipulation is essential in calculus for differentiating and integrating exponential functions.

4.8 Scientific Notation

Scientific notation expresses very large or very small numbers as a product of a number between 1 and 10 and a power of 10.

Why Scientific Notation? It makes calculations with extreme values manageable and clearly shows the order of magnitude. The distance to the sun is about \(1.5 \times 10^8\) km, and the size of an atom is about \(1 \times 10^{-10}\) m.

Scientific Notation Operations

To multiply: \((a \times 10^m) \times (b \times 10^n) = (ab) \times 10^{m+n}\)

To divide: \(\frac{a \times 10^m}{b \times 10^n} = \frac{a}{b} \times 10^{m-n}\)
