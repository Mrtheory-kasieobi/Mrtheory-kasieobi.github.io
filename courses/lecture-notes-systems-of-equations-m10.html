Lecture 7: Systems of Linear Equations

Mathematics 10 - Chapter 7

7.1 Solving Systems by Graphing

A system of linear equations consists of two or more linear equations with the same variables. The solution is the point where the lines intersect.

Why Systems Matter: Many real-world problems involve multiple constraints. For example, if you have $100 to spend on two types of items, you need to find the combination that satisfies both your budget and your needs. This is a system of equations.

Types of Solutions

One Solution: Lines intersect at exactly one point (consistent, independent)

No Solution: Lines are parallel and never intersect (inconsistent)

Infinitely Many Solutions: Lines are the same (consistent, dependent)

Example 1: Graphing a System

Solve: \(y = 2x + 1\) and \(y = -x + 4\)

Graph both lines. They intersect at approximately (1, 3).

Check: \(3 = 2(1) + 1\) ✓ and \(3 = -(1) + 4\) ✓

Motivation: This is used in business to find break-even points where revenue equals cost.

7.2 Solving Systems by Substitution

The substitution method involves solving one equation for one variable and substituting into the other equation.

When to Use Substitution: When one equation is already solved for a variable, or when coefficients are 1 or -1, making substitution straightforward.

Substitution Method Steps
  1. Solve one equation for one variable (if not already done)
  2. Substitute this expression into the other equation
  3. Solve the resulting single-variable equation
  4. Substitute back to find the other variable
  5. Check the solution in both original equations
Example 2: Substitution Method

Solve: \(y = 3x - 2\) and \(2x + y = 8\)

Substitute: \(2x + (3x - 2) = 8\)

\(5x - 2 = 8\), so \(x = 2\)

Then \(y = 3(2) - 2 = 4\)

Solution: (2, 4)

Motivation: This is used in chemistry to find concentrations in mixtures and in economics to find equilibrium points.

7.3 Solving Systems by Elimination

The elimination method involves adding or subtracting equations to eliminate one variable.

Why Elimination Works: If \(a = b\) and \(c = d\), then \(a + c = b + d\). This allows us to combine equations strategically to eliminate variables.

Elimination Method Steps
  1. Multiply one or both equations to get opposite coefficients for one variable
  2. Add the equations to eliminate that variable
  3. Solve for the remaining variable
  4. Substitute back to find the eliminated variable
  5. Check the solution in both original equations
Example 3: Elimination Method

Solve: \(3x + 2y = 12\) and \(5x - 2y = 8\)

Add equations: \(8x = 20\), so \(x = 2.5\)

Substitute: \(3(2.5) + 2y = 12\), so \(y = 3\)

Solution: (2.5, 3)

Motivation: This is used in engineering to solve for forces in structures and in finance to find portfolio allocations.

7.4 Solving Systems with Fractions and Decimals

When equations have fractions or decimals, multiply to clear them before solving.

Strategy: Multiply each equation by the LCD of all fractions to work with integers, making arithmetic easier and less error-prone.

Example 4: Clearing Fractions

Solve: \(\frac{1}{2}x + \frac{1}{3}y = 2\) and \(\frac{1}{4}x - y = -1\)

Multiply first by 6: \(3x + 2y = 12\)

Multiply second by 4: \(x - 4y = -4\)

Now solve using elimination or substitution.

Motivation: This technique is used in chemistry for balancing equations and in physics for solving equations with many constants.

7.5 Real-World Applications of Systems

Systems of equations model situations with multiple constraints or conditions.

Common Applications:

  • Mixture Problems: Finding amounts of different solutions to mix
  • Investment Problems: Finding amounts invested at different rates
  • Distance Problems: Finding speeds or times for different routes
  • Break-Even Analysis: Finding when revenue equals cost
Example 5: Investment Problem

$5000 is invested in two accounts paying 3% and 5% interest. If total interest is $210, how much is in each account?

Let \(x\) = amount at 3%, \(y\) = amount at 5%

\(x + y = 5000\) and \(0.03x + 0.05y = 210\)

Solving: \(x = 2000\), \(y = 3000\)

Motivation: This is used in personal finance and business planning to optimize returns.

7.6 Math Lab: Exploring Systems

In this lab, you'll use graphing technology to explore how changing coefficients affects the solution to a system.

Key Insights: Small changes in coefficients can dramatically change the solution. This is important in numerical analysis and computer modeling, where rounding errors can accumulate.

7.7 Solving Systems with Three Variables

Systems with three variables can be solved using elimination, working with pairs of equations to reduce to a 2-variable system.

Application: Three-variable systems appear in chemistry for balancing complex reactions, in economics for market equilibrium with three products, and in engineering for 3D force analysis.

Example 6: Three-Variable System

Solve: \(x + y + z = 6\), \(2x - y + z = 3\), \(x + 2y - z = 2\)

Using elimination: Solution is (1, 2, 3)

Motivation: This extends to n-variable systems used in computer algorithms, optimization, and data science.
