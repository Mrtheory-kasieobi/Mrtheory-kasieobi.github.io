Problem Set 10: Permutations, Combinations, Binomial Theorem

Precalculus 12 - Chapter 10 Solutions

Solutions

Problem 1: How many different 4-digit PINs can be created using digits 0-9 if repetition is not allowed? How many with repetition allowed?
Without repetition: P(10, 4) = \(\frac{10!}{(10-4)!} = \frac{10!}{6!} = 10 \times 9 \times 8 \times 7 = 5040\)
With repetition: \(10^4 = 10,000\)
Answer: Without repetition: 5040 PINs; With repetition: 10,000 PINs
Problem 2: In how many ways can 5 people be seated around a circular table? How many ways if they sit in a row?
Circular arrangement: (n-1)! for n distinct objects
Circular: \((5-1)! = 4! = 24\) ways
Linear arrangement: \(5! = 120\) ways
Answer: Circular: 24 ways; Linear: 120 ways
Problem 3: From 8 letters and 4 digits, how many license plates can be made with 2 letters followed by 3 digits? How many with 3 letters followed by 2 digits?
2 letters + 3 digits: \(P(8, 2) \times P(4, 3) = 56 \times 24 = 1344\)
3 letters + 2 digits: \(P(8, 3) \times P(4, 2) = 336 \times 12 = 4032\)
Answer: 2L+3D: 1344 plates; 3L+2D: 4032 plates
Problem 4: Expand \((2x - 1)^4\) using the binomial theorem. What is the coefficient of \(x^3\)?
Binomial theorem: \((a + b)^n = \sum_{k=0}^{n} \binom{n}{k} a^{n-k} b^k\)
Here: \(a = 2x\), \(b = -1\), \(n = 4\)
Terms: \(\binom{4}{0}(2x)^4 + \binom{4}{1}(2x)^3(-1) + \binom{4}{2}(2x)^2(-1)^2 + \binom{4}{3}(2x)(-1)^3 + \binom{4}{4}(-1)^4\)
\(= 16x^4 - 32x^3 + 24x^2 - 8x + 1\)
Coefficient of \(x^3\): \(-32\)
Answer: \(16x^4 - 32x^3 + 24x^2 - 8x + 1\), Coefficient: -32
Problem 5: A coin is flipped 10 times. What is the probability of getting exactly 6 heads? What is the probability of getting at least 8 heads?
Total outcomes: \(2^{10} = 1024\)
Exactly 6 heads: \(P = \frac{\binom{10}{6}}{1024} = \frac{210}{1024} = \frac{105}{512}\)
At least 8 heads = 8 or 9 or 10 heads
\(P = \frac{\binom{10}{8} + \binom{10}{9} + \binom{10}{10}}{1024} = \frac{45 + 10 + 1}{1024} = \frac{56}{1024} = \frac{7}{128}\)
Answer: Exactly 6: \(\frac{105}{512} \approx 0.205\); At least 8: \(\frac{7}{128} \approx 0.055\)
Problem 6: A committee of 4 is chosen from 6 men and 5 women. How many ways if there must be at least 2 women? At most 1 woman?
At least 2 women: 2W+2M or 3W+1M or 4W+0M
\(\binom{5}{2}\binom{6}{2} + \binom{5}{3}\binom{6}{1} + \binom{5}{4}\binom{6}{0}\)
\(= 10 \times 15 + 10 \times 6 + 5 \times 1 = 150 + 60 + 5 = 215\)
At most 1 woman: 0W+4M or 1W+3M
\(\binom{5}{0}\binom{6}{4} + \binom{5}{1}\binom{6}{3}\)
\(= 1 \times 15 + 5 \times 20 = 15 + 100 = 115\)
Answer: At least 2 women: 215 ways; At most 1 woman: 115 ways
Problem 7: Find the 5th term in the expansion of \((x + 2)^8\).
General term: \(T_{k+1} = \binom{8}{k} x^{8-k} \cdot 2^k\)
5th term: \(k = 4\)
\(T_5 = \binom{8}{4} x^{4} \cdot 2^4 = 70 \cdot x^4 \cdot 16 = 1120x^4\)
Answer: \(1120x^4\)
Problem 8: A pizza shop offers 8 toppings. How many different 3-topping pizzas are possible? How many total possible pizzas (any number of toppings)?
3-topping pizzas: \(\binom{8}{3} = \frac{8!}{3! \cdot 5!} = \frac{8 \times 7 \times 6}{6} = 56\)
Total pizzas: Each topping can be chosen or not: \(2^8 = 256\)
This includes the plain pizza (no toppings): 255 with at least one topping
Answer: 3-topping: 56; Total: 256 (or 255 with at least one topping)

Bonus Solutions

Bonus Problem 1: A committee of 5 is chosen from 7 men and 6 women. In how many ways can this be done if the committee must have at least 2 women and at least 1 man?
Total 11 people, need 5 with at least 2W and at least 1M
Cases: 2W+3M, 3W+2M, 4W+1M, 5W+0M (but 5W violates "at least 1M")
Valid cases: 2W+3M, 3W+2M, 4W+1M
\(\binom{6}{2}\binom{7}{3} + \binom{6}{3}\binom{7}{2} + \binom{6}{4}\binom{7}{1}\)
\(= 15 \times 35 + 20 \times 21 + 15 \times 7\)
\(= 525 + 420 + 105 = 1050\)
Answer: 1050 ways
Bonus Problem 2: Expand \((x - 1)^7\) using the binomial theorem. Find the sum of all coefficients.
Expansion: \((x - 1)^7 = \sum_{k=0}^{7} \binom{7}{k} x^{7-k} (-1)^k\)
Terms: \(x^7 - 7x^6 + 21x^5 - 35x^4 + 35x^3 - 21x^2 + 7x - 1\)
Sum of coefficients: substitute \(x = 1\):
\((1 - 1)^7 = 0^7 = 0\)
Answer: Expansion above, Sum of coefficients = 0
