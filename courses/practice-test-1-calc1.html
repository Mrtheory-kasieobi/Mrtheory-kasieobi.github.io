Worked Solutions

Each solution gives one route and, where useful, an alternative. If your work used a different valid method, you should still receive credit; the grader accepts common equivalent forms.

  1. 1. Factor: \(\frac{x^2-4}{x-2}=\frac{(x-2)(x+2)}{x-2}=x+2\to4\). Alternative: once L'Hôpital's Rule is available, differentiate numerator and denominator to get \(\frac{2x}{1}\to4\).
  2. 2. Apply the power rule term by term: \(f'(x)=12x^3-15x^2+2\). There is no extra \(+2x\) because the derivative of \(2x\) is \(2\).
  3. 3. With \(u=3x\), \(\frac{d}{dx}\sin u=\cos u\cdot u'=3\cos(3x)\).
  4. 4. \(f'(x)=3x^2-6x=3x(x-2)\), so the critical numbers are \(x=0,2\). Alternative: solve \(3x^2-6x=0\) with the quadratic formula.
  5. 5. \(\int_0^1 x^2\,dx=\left[\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1=\frac13\). Alternative: the limit of right-endpoint Riemann sums also gives \(\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^n\frac{i^2}{n^3}=\frac13\).
  6. 6. \(m=f'(3)=6\) and the point is \((3,9)\). Point-slope form gives \(y-9=6(x-3)\), i.e. \(y=6x-9\).
  7. 7. This is the standard limit \(\lim_{x\to0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\), provable by the squeeze theorem from \(\cos x\le\frac{\sin x}{x}\le1\).
  8. 8. Chain rule: \(\frac{d}{dx}e^{2x}=e^{2x}\cdot2=2e^{2x}\).
  9. 9. Average value \(=\frac{1}{2-0}\int_0^2x^2\,dx=\frac12\cdot\frac83=\frac43\).
  10. 10. \(\int\frac{dx}{x}=\ln|x|+C\). The absolute value is needed so the antiderivative works for \(x>0\) and \(x \lt 0\).
  11. 11. Let \(u=x^2+1\). Then \(du=2x\,dx\), so \(f'(x)=\frac{2x}{x^2+1}\). Alternative: logarithmic differentiation of \(f=x^2+1\) gives the same factor \(2x/(x^2+1)\).
  12. 12. \(\int_0^2(3x^2-4x+1)\,dx=[x^3-2x^2+x]_0^2=8-8+2=2\).
  13. 13. \(\frac{d}{dx}x^2=2x\) and \(\frac{d}{dx}\tan^{-1}x=\frac{1}{1+x^2}\), so the derivative is \(2x+\frac{1}{1+x^2}\).
  14. 14. \(f'(x)=3(x^2-1)\), so \(x=\pm1\). Since \(f''(x)=6x\), \(f''(-1)\lt0\) gives a local maximum at \(-1\) and \(f''(1)\gt0\) gives a local minimum at \(1\). Alternative: a sign chart for \(f'\) has signs \(+,-,+\), giving the same classification.
  15. 15. Use \(\sin^2x=\frac{1-\cos 2x}{2}\): \(\int_0^\pi\sin^2x\,dx=\left[\frac{x}{2}-\frac{\sin2x}{4}\right]_0^\pi=\frac{\pi}{2}\). Alternative: over a full half-period the average value of \(\sin^2x\) is \(1/2\).
  16. 16. \(y(2)=4-6=-2\) and \(y'(2)=4-3=1\), so \(y+2=1(x-2)\), i.e. \(y=x-4\).
  17. 17. \(\int(4x-1)\,dx=2x^2-x+C\). Differentiate to check: \(4x-1\).
  18. 18. On \([0,1]\), \(x\ge x^2\), so \(A=\int_0^1(x-x^2)\,dx=\left[\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^3}{3}\right]_0^1=\frac16\). Alternative (horizontal slices): \(\int_0^1(\sqrt y-y)\,dy=\frac23-\frac12=\frac16\).
  19. 19. Washers about the \(x\)-axis: \(V=\pi\int_0^2(x^2)^2\,dx=\pi\left[\frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^2=\frac{32\pi}{5}\). Alternative (shells): \(2\pi\int_0^2 y(2-\sqrt y)\,dy=\frac{32\pi}{5}\).
  20. 20. Substitute \(u=2x\): \(\int\cos(2x)\,dx=\frac12\sin(2x)+C\).
