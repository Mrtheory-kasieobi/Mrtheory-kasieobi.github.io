Problem Set 4: Functions and Relations

Introduction to Proofs - Lecture 4 Medium

Try each problem before reading the solutions. A proof is judged by the completeness of its logical chain, not by how short it is. State your assumptions, justify every nontrivial step, and verify that the conclusion follows exactly.

Problems

Problem 1

Let \(a\sim b\) on \(\mathbb{Z}\) mean \(a\equiv b\pmod 3\). Prove \(\sim\) is an equivalence relation and list its distinct equivalence classes.

Problem 2

Give an explicit example of a relation on \(\{1,2\}\) that is symmetric and transitive but not reflexive, and explain why this does not contradict Theorem 4.5.

Problem 3

Prove that the relation \(\le\) on \(\mathbb{R}\) is a total order.

Problem 4

Prove that \(f:\mathbb{R}\setminus\{1\} o\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2\}\), \(f(x)=\frac{2x}{x-1}\) is injective, and find its range. Is it surjective onto \(\mathbb{R}\setminus\{2\}\)?

Problem 5

Let \(f:A o B\), and let \(S,T\subseteq A\). Prove \(f(S\cup T)=f(S)\cup f(T)\), and give an example where \(f(S\cap T) e f(S)\cap f(T)\).

Problem 6

Prove part (2) of Theorem 4.12: if \(f:A o B\) and \(g:B o C\) are surjective, then \(g\circ f\) is surjective.

Problem 7

A function \(f:A o B\) satisfies \(f^{-1}(\{b\})=\{a\}\) for every \(b\in B\) and a fixed \(a\in A\). What can you conclude about \(A\), \(B\), and \(f\)? Justify.

Problem 8

Prove that if \(g\circ f\) is injective, then \(f\) is injective. Give a counterexample to the claim that \(g\) must also be injective.

Bonus Problem

Prove that if \(f:A\to B\) and \(g:B\to C\) are injective, then \(g\circ f:A\to C\) is injective.
