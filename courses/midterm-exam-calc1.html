Calculus I Midterm Exam

Calculus I · 30 marks + 5 bonus

This exam contains 20 questions: Part A (10 multiple choice, 1 mark each) and Part B (10 written response, 2 marks each), followed by Part C (one bonus problem worth 5 marks).

Total: 30 graded marks plus a 5-mark bonus that is reported separately and does not change your percentage. Suggested time: 75 minutes. The problems are deliberately challenging; every one can be solved with the techniques from the first half of Calculus I. After submitting, you will see the correct choices, expected answer forms, and full worked solutions with alternative methods.

Midterm Exam - Calculus I

Part A: Multiple Choice (1 mark each)

Choose the best answer for each question. Each question has exactly one correct choice.

1.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0} \frac{\sqrt{1+2x}-\sqrt{1-2x}}{\sin 3x}\).

2.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to -\infty}\left(\sqrt{x^2-6x}+x\right)\).

3.

Let \(f(x)=x\sin(1/x)\) for \(x \ne 0\) and \(f(0)=0\). Which statement is true?

4.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{h \to 0}\frac{\ln(3+h)-\ln 3}{h}\).

5.

The curve \(x^3+y^3=9xy\) passes through \((2,4)\). Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) at that point.

6.

Let \(y=x^{\sin x}\). Find \(y'\) at \(x=\frac{\pi}{2}\).

7.

A \(13\)-ft ladder leans against a wall. Its foot slides away from the wall at \(2\) ft/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the foot is \(5\) ft from the wall?

8.

The Mean Value Theorem applied to \(f(x)=\sin x\) on \([0,\pi]\) guarantees a \(c \in (0,\pi)\) with \(f'(c)=0\). One such \(c\) is

9.

A window is a rectangle of height \(h\) topped by a semicircle of radius \(r\) (so the rectangle has width \(2r\)). Its total perimeter is \(10\) m. Which \(r\) maximizes the area?

10.

Let \(f(x)=x^3+x\). Find \(\left(f^{-1}\right)'(2)\).

Part B: Written Response (2 marks each)

Show all of your work in the box provided. Exact answers are required.

11.

Evaluate \(\displaystyle\lim_{x \to 0}\frac{1-\cos(3x)}{x\sin(2x)}\). Use only the standard trigonometric limits.

12.

Let \(f(x)=ax^2+b\) for \(x \lt 1\) and \(f(x)=3x+1\) for \(x \ge 1\). Find all \(a,b\) for which \(f\) is differentiable at \(x=1\).

13.

The curve \(x^2y+y^3=10\) contains \((1,2)\). Find the equation of its tangent line at that point.

14.

If \(f(x)=x^2e^{3x}\), find \(f''(x)\).

15.

Find the \(x\)-coordinates of all inflection points of \(f(x)=x^4-4x^3\). Justify the sign change of \(f''\).

16.

A \(12\) cm by \(12\) cm sheet of cardboard has equal squares of side \(x\) cut from its corners, and the flaps are folded up to make an open box. Find the cut size \(x\) that maximizes the volume and state that maximum volume.

17.

Water is poured into an inverted conical tank at \(2\) cm\(^3\)/s. The tank has height \(10\) cm and top radius \(5\) cm, so the water's radius is half its depth \(h\). How fast is the water level rising when \(h=4\) cm?

18.

Use the Mean Value Theorem to find the number \(c \in (1,e)\) for \(f(x)=\ln x\) on \([1,e]\).

19.

Use the limit definition of the derivative to find \(f'(2)\) for \(f(x)=\frac{1}{x+1}\).

20.

Use a linear approximation based at \(x=8\) to estimate \(\sqrt[3]{8.06}\). Give an exact decimal.

Part C: Bonus Problem (5 marks, reported separately)

Challenge problem. It is graded, but its marks are not added to the 30-mark total.

21 (bonus).

Suppose \(f\) is differentiable everywhere, \(f(0)=1\), \(f'(0)=2\), and \(f(x+y)=f(x)f(y)\) for all real \(x,y\).

(a) Use the limit definition of the derivative to prove that \(f'(x)=2f(x)\). (b) If \(f(1)=e^2\), find \(f(3)\).

Worked Solutions

Each solution includes a standard method and an alternative route where one is natural. A different but valid approach earns full credit; the grader accepts common equivalent forms and key intermediate results.

  1. 1. Rationalize the numerator: \(\frac{\sqrt{1+2x}-\sqrt{1-2x}}{\sin3x}\cdot\frac{\sqrt{1+2x}+\sqrt{1-2x}}{\sqrt{1+2x}+\sqrt{1-2x}}=\frac{4x}{\sin3x(\sqrt{1+2x}+\sqrt{1-2x})}\to\frac{4}{3\cdot2}=\frac23\). Alternative: recognize the numerator's derivative at \(0\) as \(2\) and the denominator's as \(3\), giving \(2/3\).
  2. 2. For \(x\to-\infty\), write \(x=-t\), \(t\to\infty\): \(\sqrt{t^2+6t}-t=\frac{6t}{\sqrt{t^2+6t}+t}\to3\). Alternative: rationalize directly and divide numerator and denominator by \(-x\), which is positive.
  3. 3. \(|x\sin(1/x)|\le|x|\to0\), so \(f\) is continuous at \(0\). But \(\frac{f(h)-f(0)}{h}=\sin(1/h)\) has no limit, so \(f'(0)\) does not exist. Alternative: take \(h_n=1/(2\pi n)\) and \(k_n=1/((2n+\tfrac12)\pi)\) to get two different difference-quotient limits.
  4. 4. This is the derivative of \(\ln x\) at \(x=3\): \(\frac1{3}\). Alternative: \(\ln(3+h)-\ln3=\ln(1+h/3)\sim h/3\).
  5. 5. Differentiate implicitly: \(3x^2+3y^2y'=9y+9xy'\), so \(y'=\frac{9y-3x^2}{3y^2-9x}\). At \((2,4)\), \(y'=\frac{36-12}{48-18}=\frac{24}{30}=\frac45\).
  6. 6. Logarithmic differentiation: \(\ln y=\sin x\ln x\), so \(y'/y=\cos x\ln x+\frac{\sin x}{x}\). At \(x=\pi/2\), \(y=\pi/2\) and \(y'=(\pi/2)\cdot(2/\pi)=1\). Alternative: write \(y=e^{\sin x\ln x}\) and apply the chain rule.
  7. 7. \(x^2+y^2=169\), so \(2x x'+2y y'=0\). When \(x=5\), \(y=12\), hence \(y'=-\frac{x}{y}x'=-\frac5{12}\cdot2=-\frac56\) ft/s; the top moves down at \(5/6\) ft/s. Alternative: differentiate \(y=\sqrt{169-x^2}\) directly.
  8. 8. The secant slope is \(\frac{0-0}{\pi}=0\), and \(f'(c)=\cos c=0\) gives \(c=\pi/2\). This is also the conclusion of Rolle's Theorem.
  9. 9. Perimeter: \(2h+2r+\pi r=10\), so \(h=\frac{10-(2+\pi)r}{2}\). Area \(A=2rh+\frac{\pi r^2}{2}=10r-(2+\frac{\pi}{2})r^2\). Then \(A'=10-(4+\pi)r=0\), giving \(r=\frac{10}{4+\pi}\). Alternative: differentiate the area after substituting \(h\); this is the same one-variable reduction.
  10. 10. Let \(a=f^{-1}(2)\). Then \(a^3+a=2\), so \(a=1\). Since \(f'(x)=3x^2+1\), \((f^{-1})'(2)=\frac{1}{f'(1)}=\frac14\). Alternative: differentiate \(x=y^3+y\) implicitly to get \(dx/dy=3y^2+1=4\).
  11. 11. \(1-\cos(3x)=2\sin^2(3x/2)\), so the limit is \(\frac{2(3/2)^2}{2}=\frac94\). Alternative: use \(1-\cos u\sim u^2/2\) and \(\sin v\sim v\) as \(u,v\to0\).
  12. 12. Continuity at \(1\): \(a+b=4\). Matching one-sided derivatives: \(2a=3\). Hence \(a=\frac32\), \(b=\frac52\).
  13. 13. Differentiate: \(2xy+x^2y'+3y^2y'=0\), so \(y'=-\frac{2xy}{x^2+3y^2}\). At \((1,2)\), \(y'=-\frac4{13}\). Tangent: \(y-2=-\frac4{13}(x-1)\).
  14. 14. \(f'=(3x^2+2x)e^{3x}\); differentiating again gives \(f''=(9x^2+12x+2)e^{3x}\). Alternative: use the Leibniz rule for the product \(x^2\cdot e^{3x}\).
  15. 15. \(f'=4x^2(x-3)\) and \(f''=12x(x-2)\). The sign of \(f''\) is \(+,-,+\) on \((-\infty,0),(0,2),(2,\infty)\), so both \(x=0\) and \(x=2\) are inflection \(x\)-coordinates.
  16. 16. \(V=x(12-2x)^2\), so \(V'=(12-2x)(12-6x)\). The valid critical point is \(x=2\), giving \(V=2\cdot8^2=128\) cm\(^3\).
  17. 17. Similar triangles give \(r=h/2\), so \(V=\frac{\pi}{3}(h/2)^2h=\frac{\pi}{12}h^3\). Then \(\frac{dV}{dt}=\frac{\pi}{4}h^2\frac{dh}{dt}\). At \(h=4\), \(\frac{dh}{dt}=\frac{2}{4\pi}=\frac{1}{2\pi}\) cm/s. Alternative: differentiate \(V=\frac{\pi}{3}r^2h\) after substituting \(r=h/2\).
  18. 18. The secant slope is \(\frac{\ln e-\ln1}{e-1}=\frac1{e-1}\). Since \(f'(c)=1/c\), \(c=e-1\).
  19. 19. \(f'(2)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{\frac1{3+h}-\frac13}{h}=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{-h}{3h(3+h)}=-\frac19\). Alternative: the derivative formula \(-1/(x+1)^2\) gives \(-1/9\) at \(x=2\).
  20. 20. With \(f(x)=x^{1/3}\), \(f(8)=2\) and \(f'(8)=\frac{1}{3\cdot8^{2/3}}=\frac1{12}\). Thus \(\sqrt[3]{8.06}\approx2+\frac{0.06}{12}=2.005\).
  21. 21 (bonus). \(f'(x)=\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}=f(x)\lim_{h\to0}\frac{f(h)-1}{h}=f(x)f'(0)=2f(x)\). Also \(f(3)=f(1+1+1)=f(1)^3=(e^2)^3=e^6\). Alternative: after proving \(f'=2f\), integration gives \(f(x)=e^{2x}\), and \(f(3)=e^6\).
