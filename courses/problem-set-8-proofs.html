Problem Set 8: Strong Induction and Well-Ordering

Introduction to Proofs - Lecture 8 Medium

Try each problem before reading the solutions. A proof is judged by the completeness of its logical chain, not by how short it is. State your assumptions, justify every nontrivial step, and verify that the conclusion follows exactly.

Problems

Problem 1

Prove by strong induction that every integer \(n\ge2\) is a sum of copies of \(2\) and \(3\).

Problem 2

Prove by strong induction that every integer \(n\ge2\) has a prime divisor.

Problem 3

Determine whether the Fibonacci numbers satisfy \(a_n\ge n\) for all \(n\ge1\); if not, give a correct linear lower bound and prove it.

Problem 4

Prove by strong induction that for \(n\ge1\), \(n\) can be written uniquely as \(2^a\cdot q\) with \(q\) odd.

Problem 5

Use well-ordering to prove: if \(S\subseteq\mathbb{N}\) and \(n\in S\) implies \(n+1\in S\), and \(1\in S\), then \(S=\mathbb{N}\).

Problem 6

Define \(b_0=0,b_1=1,b_2=2\), \(b_{n+3}=b_{n+2}+b_{n+1}+b_n\). Prove \(b_n\le3^n\) for all \(n\ge0\).

Problem 7

Explain the flaw: "For strong induction on \(P(n)\), assume \(P(k)\) for all \(k<n\); since \(P(n)\) follows from \(P(n-1)\), no base case is needed."

Problem 8

Prove by strong induction that \(3^n\) can be written as a sum of \(n\) odd positive integers for every \(n\ge1\).

Bonus Problem

Prove by strong induction that \(2^n>n^2\) for every integer \(n\ge5\).
