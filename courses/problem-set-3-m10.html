Problem Set 3: Factors and Products

Mathematics 10 - Chapter 3 Medium

Problems

Problem 1

Find the prime factorization of \(120\) and use it to list all factors.

Problem 2

Factor completely: \(x^2 + 9x + 20\)

Problem 3

Factor: \(2x^2 + 7x - 15\) (Use AC method: find two numbers that multiply to \(-30\) and add to \(7\))

Problem 4

Factor: \(9x^2 - 16y^2\) (Difference of squares pattern: \(a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)\))

Problem 5

Factor: \(x^2 - 10x + 25\) (Perfect square trinomial: \(a^2 - 2ab + b^2 = (a-b)^2\))

Problem 6

Factor: \(3x^3 - 12x\) (First factor out the GCF)

Problem 7

Solve: \(x^2 + 7x + 12 = 0\) by factoring

Problem 8

Factor: \(\frac{1}{2}x^2 + \frac{3}{4}x + \frac{1}{8}\) (Multiply by LCD first)

Bonus Problems

Bonus Problem 1 Hard

Factor Frenzy: A rectangle's area is modeled by \(x^4 - 16\) square units. Factor this expression completely, then explain why this factorization reveals that the rectangle can only have integer dimensions when \(x\) is an even number. Find all possible integer dimensions when \(x = 6\).

Bonus Problem 2 Medium

Perfect Square Chain: Find the smallest positive integer \(n\) such that \(n\), \(n+1\), and \(n+2\) are all perfect squares, or prove that no such integer exists. (Hint: Consider the gaps between consecutive perfect squares.)

Bonus Problem 3 Hard

Triangle Puzzle: A right triangle has legs that differ by 3 units. The area is 54 square units more than the square of the shorter leg. Find the dimensions of the triangle. Set up and solve a quadratic equation, then use factoring to find integer solutions.
