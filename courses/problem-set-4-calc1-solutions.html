Problem Set 4: Chain Rule and Implicit Differentiation - SOLUTIONS

Calculus I - Week 4

Solution Guide

Note: These are detailed solutions showing all steps and reasoning. Each step is clearly justified.

Problem 1: Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = \sin(x^3 + 2x)\)

Solution:
Step 1: Identify the chain rule structure
Outer function: \(\sin(u)\), where \(u = x^3 + 2x\)
Step 2: Apply the chain rule
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos(u) \cdot \frac{du}{dx}\)
Step 3: Differentiate the inner function
\(\frac{d}{dx}[x^3 + 2x] = 3x^2 + 2\)
Step 4: Combine results
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos(x^3 + 2x) \cdot (3x^2 + 2) = (3x^2 + 2)\cos(x^3 + 2x)\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{(3x^2 + 2)\cos(x^3 + 2x)}\)

Problem 2: Find \(\frac{d}{dx}[e^{\tan(x^2)}]\)

Solution:
Step 1: Identify the nested structure
Three layers: \(e^u\), where \(u = \tan(v)\), where \(v = x^2\)
Step 2: Apply chain rule repeatedly
\(\frac{d}{dx} = \frac{d}{du}[e^u] \cdot \frac{d}{dv}[\tan v] \cdot \frac{d}{dx}[x^2]\)
Step 3: Compute each derivative
\(\frac{d}{du}[e^u] = e^u\), \(\frac{d}{dv}[\tan v] = \sec^2 v\), \(\frac{d}{dx}[x^2] = 2x\)
Step 4: Substitute back
\(\frac{d}{dx} = e^{\tan(x^2)} \cdot \sec^2(x^2) \cdot 2x\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{2xe^{\tan(x^2)}\sec^2(x^2)}\)

Problem 3: Find \(\frac{d}{dx}[\ln(\sin(x^2))]\)

Solution:
Step 1: Outer function is \(\ln(u)\)
where \(u = \sin(x^2)\)
Step 2: Apply chain rule
\(\frac{d}{dx} = \frac{1}{\sin(x^2)} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x^2)]\)
Step 3: Differentiate \(\sin(x^2)\)
\(\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x^2)] = \cos(x^2) \cdot 2x = 2x\cos(x^2)\)
Step 4: Combine
\(\frac{d}{dx} = \frac{2x\cos(x^2)}{\sin(x^2)} = 2x\cot(x^2)\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{2x\cot(x^2)}\)

Problem 4: If \(y = \sqrt[3]{x^2 + 1}\), find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\)

Solution:
Step 1: Rewrite using fractional exponent
\(y = (x^2 + 1)^{1/3}\)
Step 2: Apply chain rule
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{3}(x^2 + 1)^{-2/3} \cdot \frac{d}{dx}[x^2 + 1]\)
Step 3: Differentiate inner function
\(\frac{d}{dx}[x^2 + 1] = 2x\)
Step 4: Combine and simplify
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2x}{3}(x^2 + 1)^{-2/3} = \frac{2x}{3(x^2 + 1)^{2/3}}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{2x}{3(x^2 + 1)^{2/3}}}\)

Problem 5: Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = (2x^3 - 5)^{-4}\)

Solution:
Step 1: Identify outer and inner functions
Outer: \(u^{-4}\), Inner: \(u = 2x^3 - 5\)
Step 2: Apply chain rule
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = -4u^{-5} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}\)
Step 3: Find \(\frac{du}{dx}\)
\(\frac{d}{dx}[2x^3 - 5] = 6x^2\)
Step 4: Substitute back
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = -4(2x^3 - 5)^{-5} \cdot 6x^2 = -\frac{24x^2}{(2x^3 - 5)^5}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{-\frac{24x^2}{(2x^3 - 5)^5}}\)

Problem 6: Use implicit differentiation for \(x^2y + y^3 = 6\)

Solution:
Step 1: Differentiate both sides with respect to \(x\)
\(\frac{d}{dx}[x^2y + y^3] = \frac{d}{dx}[6]\)
Step 2: Apply product rule to \(x^2y\)
\(\frac{d}{dx}[x^2y] = 2x \cdot y + x^2 \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}\)
Step 3: Apply chain rule to \(y^3\)
\(\frac{d}{dx}[y^3] = 3y^2 \cdot \frac{dy}{dx}\)
Step 4: Right side derivative
\(\frac{d}{dx}[6] = 0\)
Step 5: Combine equation
\(2xy + x^2\frac{dy}{dx} + 3y^2\frac{dy}{dx} = 0\)
Step 6: Collect \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) terms
\(\frac{dy}{dx}(x^2 + 3y^2) = -2xy\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx} = -\frac{2xy}{x^2 + 3y^2}}\)

Problem 7: Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for \(\sin(x+y) = x + y\)

Solution:
Step 1: Differentiate both sides
\(\frac{d}{dx}[\sin(x+y)] = \frac{d}{dx}[x+y]\)
Step 2: Left side using chain rule
\(\cos(x+y) \cdot (1 + \frac{dy}{dx})\)
Step 3: Right side
\(1 + \frac{dy}{dx}\)
Step 4: Set equal and solve
\(\cos(x+y)(1 + \frac{dy}{dx}) = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx}\)
Step 5: Expand and collect
\(\cos(x+y) + \cos(x+y)\frac{dy}{dx} = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx}\)
\(\cos(x+y)\frac{dy}{dx} - \frac{dy}{dx} = 1 - \cos(x+y)\)
Step 6: Factor and solve
\(\frac{dy}{dx}[\cos(x+y) - 1] = 1 - \cos(x+y)\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx} = -1}\) (when \(\cos(x+y) \neq 1\))

Problem 8: A spherical balloon is being inflated. If the radius increases at 2 cm/s, how fast is the volume increasing when \(r = 10\) cm?

Solution:
Step 1: Write the volume formula
Volume of sphere: \(V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\)
Step 2: Differentiate with respect to time
\(\frac{dV}{dt} = \frac{4}{3}\pi \cdot 3r^2 \cdot \frac{dr}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \frac{dr}{dt}\)
Step 3: Substitute given values
\(r = 10\) cm, \(\frac{dr}{dt} = 2\) cm/s
Step 4: Calculate
\(\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi (10)^2 (2) = 4\pi \cdot 100 \cdot 2 = 800\pi\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{800\pi \text{ cm}^3/\text{s}}\)

Problem 9: Find the equation of the tangent line to \(y = e^{x^2}\) at \(x = 1\)

Solution:
Step 1: Find the point of tangency
\(y = e^{1^2} = e\), so point is \((1, e)\)
Step 2: Find the slope using derivative
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = e^{x^2} \cdot 2x\) (chain rule)
Step 3: Evaluate slope at x = 1
\(\frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{x=1} = e^{1} \cdot 2(1) = 2e\)
Step 4: Use point-slope form
\(y - e = 2e(x - 1)\)
Step 5: Simplify
\(y = 2ex - 2e + e = 2ex - e\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{y = 2ex - e}\)

Problem 10: Find \(\frac{d}{dx}[x^{x}]\) using logarithmic differentiation

Solution:
Step 1: Take natural logarithm of both sides
Let \(y = x^x\). Then \(\ln y = \ln(x^x) = x\ln x\)
Step 2: Differentiate implicitly
\(\frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}[x\ln x]\)
Step 3: Apply product rule on right
\(\frac{d}{dx}[x\ln x] = 1 \cdot \ln x + x \cdot \frac{1}{x} = \ln x + 1\)
Step 4: Solve for \(\frac{dy}{dx}\)
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = y(\ln x + 1) = x^x(\ln x + 1)\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{x^x(\ln x + 1)}\)

Bonus 1: Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = (\sin x)^{\cos x}\)

Solution:
Step 1: Logarithmic differentiation
\(\ln y = \cos x \cdot \ln(\sin x)\)
Step 2: Differentiate both sides
\(\frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}[\cos x \cdot \ln(\sin x)]\)
Step 3: Product rule on right
\(= (-\sin x)\ln(\sin x) + \cos x \cdot \frac{\cos x}{\sin x}\)
Step 4: Simplify
\(\frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = -\sin x \ln(\sin x) + \cot x \cos x\)
Step 5: Multiply by y
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = (\sin x)^{\cos x}[-\sin x \ln(\sin x) + \cot x \cos x]\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{(\sin x)^{\cos x}[-\sin x \ln(\sin x) + \cot x \cos x]}\)

Bonus 2: Prove that if \(f(g(x)) = x\) for all \(x\), then \(g(f(x)) = x\) for all \(x\) in the appropriate domains

Solution:
Step 1: Understand the given condition
\(f(g(x)) = x\) means \(g\) is a left inverse of \(f\)
Step 2: Differentiate both sides
\(\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) = 1\)
Step 3: Conclude non-zero derivatives
Since the product equals 1, neither factor can be zero: \(f'(g(x)) \neq 0\) and \(g'(x) \neq 0\)
Step 4: Apply Inverse Function Theorem
Since \(f'(g(x)) \neq 0\), \(f\) is locally invertible with inverse \(g\).
Therefore, \(g(f(x)) = x\) on the range of \(f\).
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{g(f(x)) = x \text{ on the appropriate domain}}\)
