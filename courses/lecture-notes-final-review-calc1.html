Final Review

Calculus I - Comprehensive Final Review

Complete Course Review

Big picture. Calculus I is one extended argument about change and accumulation. The limit underlies continuity, the derivative, and the integral. Differentiation rules let us compute rates of change and solve applied problems; the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus connects derivatives to integrals by showing that integration is reversed differentiation. On the final, success depends on three habits: recalling definitions precisely, checking hypotheses before invoking a theorem, and setting up applications carefully before computing.

Exam roadmap - what to prioritize:
  1. Limits: know the definition, one-sided limits, indeterminate forms, and the special limits including \(\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\).
  2. Derivatives: power, product, quotient, and chain rules; implicit and logarithmic differentiation.
  3. Theorem hypotheses: EVT, IVT, Rolle, MVT, and both parts of the FTC; state them correctly.
  4. Applications of derivatives: related rates, optimization, and curve sketching with sign charts.
  5. Integration: antiderivatives, \(u\)-substitution, integration by parts, and FTC I with variable limits.
  6. Applications of integrals: area, volumes by disk and washer and shell, and work; identify the geometry first.
  7. Communication: show setup, justify which formula applies, and give units with a final answer.

2. Limits and Continuity

Definitions. The limit \(\lim_{x\to a}f(x)=L\) means \(f(x)\) can be made arbitrarily close to \(L\) by taking \(x\) close to \(a\) but not equal to \(a\). Continuity at \(a\) requires that \(f(a)\) exists, \(\lim_{x\to a}f(x)\) exists, and the two are equal. The derivative is itself a limit: \[f'(a)=\lim_{h\to 0}\frac{f(a+h)-f(a)}{h}.\]
Special limits and indeterminate forms:
  • \(\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\) and \(\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x}=0\).
  • \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{\sin x}{x}=0\), because \(\sin x\) stays bounded.
  • \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{1}{x}\right)^x=e\) and \(\lim_{x\to 0}(1+x)^{1/x}=e\).
  • Forms \(0/0\), \(\infty/\infty\), \(\infty-\infty\), \(0\cdot\infty\), \(1^{\infty}\) require more work: factor, rationalize, combine fractions, or use a known limit.
Model example 2.1. Evaluate \(\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{\sin(5x)}{\sin(3x)}\).

Rewrite as \(\frac{\sin(5x)}{5x}\cdot\frac{3x}{\sin(3x)}\cdot\frac{5}{3}\). The first two factors approach \(1\), so the limit is \(\frac{5}{3}\).

Model example 2.2 (one-sided limits). Let \(f(x)=x+1\) for \(x\le 0\) and \(f(x)=x^2\) for \(x \gt 0\). Decide whether \(\lim_{x\to 0}f(x)\) exists.

The left-hand limit is \(\lim_{x\to 0^-}(x+1)=1\), while the right-hand limit is \(\lim_{x\to 0^+}x^2=0\). Because these differ, the two-sided limit does not exist. Note that \(f(0)=1\), which agrees with the left limit only; a function value cannot repair a jump between one-sided limits.

Model example 2.3 (squeeze). Evaluate \(\lim_{x\to 0}x\sin\!\left(\frac{1}{x}\right)\).

Since \(-1\le\sin(1/x)\le 1\), we have \(-|x|\le x\sin(1/x)\le |x|\). Both bounds approach \(0\), so the Squeeze Theorem gives limit \(0\).

3. Derivative Formulas (Memorize)

\(\frac{d}{dx}[x^n]=nx^{n-1}\)\(\frac{d}{dx}[\sin x]=\cos x\)\(\frac{d}{dx}[\cos x]=-\sin x\)
\(\frac{d}{dx}[e^x]=e^x\)\(\frac{d}{dx}[\ln x]=\frac{1}{x}\)\(\frac{d}{dx}[\tan x]=\sec^2 x\)
\(\frac{d}{dx}[\arcsin x]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\)\(\frac{d}{dx}[\arctan x]=\frac{1}{1+x^2}\)\(\frac{d}{dx}[a^x]=a^x\ln a\)

For \(a^x\) assume \(a \gt 0\) and \(a\ne 1\).

Combination rules. For differentiable \(u\) and \(v\): \[\frac{d}{dx}[u v]=u'v+uv'.\] \[\frac{d}{dx}\left[\frac{u}{v}\right]=\frac{u'v-uv'}{v^2},\quad v\ne 0.\] \[\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))]=f'(g(x))\,g'(x).\] The last formula is the chain rule: the derivative of a composition is the derivative of the outside function evaluated at the inside function, multiplied by the derivative of the inside function.
Model example 3.1. Differentiate \(y=\ln\!\left(\frac{x^2+1}{x-1}\right)\).

Expand first: \(y=\ln(x^2+1)-\ln(x-1)\). Then \(y'=\frac{2x}{x^2+1}-\frac{1}{x-1}\), valid for \(x \gt 1\).

4. Integration Formulas (Memorize)

\(\int x^n\,dx=\frac{x^{n+1}}{n+1}+C,\ n\ne -1\)\(\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx=\ln|x|+C\)\(\int e^x\,dx=e^x+C\)
\(\int \sin x\,dx=-\cos x+C\)\(\int \cos x\,dx=\sin x+C\)\(\int \sec^2 x\,dx=\tan x+C\)
\(\int \frac{1}{\sqrt{1-x^2}}\,dx=\arcsin x+C\)\(\int \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx=\arctan x+C\)\(\int a^x\,dx=\frac{a^x}{\ln a}+C\)

The power rule excludes \(n=-1\) because it would divide by zero; that case is the logarithm. For \(a^x\), take \(a \gt 0\) and \(a\ne 1\). Include the differential \(dx\) and the constant \(C\) on indefinite integrals.

5. Key Theorems and Their Hypotheses

Intermediate Value Theorem. If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(N\) lies between \(f(a)\) and \(f(b)\), then some \(c\in[a,b]\) satisfies \(f(c)=N\).
Extreme Value Theorem. If \(f\) is continuous on the closed, bounded interval \([a,b]\), then \(f\) attains an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum at some points of \([a,b]\).
Fermat's Theorem. If \(f\) has a local maximum or minimum at \(c\) and \(f'(c)\) exists, then \(f'(c)=0\). Extrema can also occur where \(f'\) does not exist, so check both kinds of critical points.
Rolle's Theorem. Let \(a \lt b\). If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), differentiable on \((a,b)\), and \(f(a)=f(b)\), then there is a \(c\in(a,b)\) with \(f'(c)=0\).
Mean Value Theorem. Let \(a \lt b\). If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\), then there is a \(c\in(a,b)\) with \[f'(c)=\frac{f(b)-f(a)}{b-a}.\] All hypotheses must be verified: continuity on the closed interval, differentiability on the open interval, and \(a \lt b\).
Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, Part II. If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and \(F'=f\) on \([a,b]\), then \[\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=F(b)-F(a).\] The differential \(dx\) identifies the integration variable, and continuity of \(f\) is a genuine hypothesis.

6. FTC Part I and Variable Limits

FTC Part I. If \(f\) is continuous on an interval containing \(a\), then \(g(x)=\int_a^x f(t)\,dt\) satisfies \(g'(x)=f(x)\). With variable limits and continuous \(f\), the chain rule gives \[\frac{d}{dx}\int_{v(x)}^{u(x)}f(t)\,dt=f(u(x))\,u'(x)-f(v(x))\,v'(x).\] Notice that the integrand is evaluated at the limits, and each limit contributes its own derivative.
Model example 6.1. Find \(\frac{d}{dx}\int_{x}^{x^2}\sqrt{1+t^2}\,dt\).

Here \(u(x)=x^2\) and \(v(x)=x\). The derivative is \(\sqrt{1+(x^2)^2}\cdot 2x-\sqrt{1+x^2}\cdot 1=2x\sqrt{1+x^4}-\sqrt{1+x^2}\).

7. Applications of Derivatives

Word-problem routine: draw and label a diagram; decide which quantities change with time; write one equation valid at all times; differentiate with respect to time; substitute known values only afterward; state units and whether a rate is signed or a speed. For optimization, reduce to one variable, respect the domain including endpoints, test all critical points, and justify the maximum or minimum.
Model example 7.1 (related rates). A 10 ft ladder leans against a wall and its bottom slides away at \(2\) ft/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is \(6\) ft from the wall?

Let \(x\) be the bottom's distance from the wall and \(y\) the top's height, so \(x^2+y^2=100\). Differentiating gives \(2x\frac{dx}{dt}+2y\frac{dy}{dt}=0\). At \(x=6\), \(y=8\), hence \(24+16\frac{dy}{dt}=0\) and \(\frac{dy}{dt}=-\frac{3}{2}\) ft/s.

The negative sign indicates that \(y\) is decreasing. The top slides down at speed \(\frac{3}{2}\) ft/s, even though its signed rate of change is \(-\frac{3}{2}\) ft/s. Report the sign when the question asks for \(\frac{dy}{dt}\), and report a positive speed when it asks "how fast."

Model example 7.2 (optimization). A rectangle has perimeter \(24\) m. Find its largest possible area.

Let the sides be \(x\) and \(y\). The constraint \(2x+2y=24\) gives \(y=12-x\), so \(A(x)=x(12-x)=12x-x^2\) on \(0\le x\le 12\). Then \(A'(x)=12-2x\) gives \(x=6\). Since \(A(0)=A(12)=0\) and \(A(6)=36\), the maximum area is \(36\) m\(^2\), achieved by a \(6\) m by \(6\) m square.

Model example 7.3 (curve sketching). Sketch the main features of \(f(x)=x^3-3x\).

Intercepts: \(x(x^2-3)=0\) gives \(x=0,\pm\sqrt{3}\), and \(f\) is odd. Since \(f'(x)=3(x-1)(x+1)\), the function increases on \((-\infty,-1)\), decreases on \((-1,1)\), and increases on \((1,\infty)\). Thus \(f(-1)=2\) is a local maximum and \(f(1)=-2\) a local minimum.

Also \(f''(x)=6x\), so the graph is concave down for \(x \lt 0\) and concave up for \(x \gt 0\), with inflection point \((0,0)\). These sign charts are enough to produce a reliable sketch.

8. Integration Techniques

Substitution. Choose \(u=g(x)\) so that \(du=g'(x)\,dx\) appears in the integrand. Then \[\int f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx=\int f(u)\,du=F(g(x))+C.\] For a definite integral, replace the limits by \(g(a)\) and \(g(b)\) when changing variables.
Integration by parts. From the product rule, \[\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du.\] Choose \(u\) to be a factor that simplifies when differentiated (logarithms and inverse trig functions are good choices) and \(dv\) a factor you can integrate.
Model example 8.1. Evaluate \(\int_0^{\pi/4}\frac{\tan x}{\cos^2 x}\,dx\).

Since \(\frac{1}{\cos^2 x}=\sec^2 x\), the integral is \(\int_0^{\pi/4}\tan x\sec^2 x\,dx\). Let \(u=\tan x\), so \(du=\sec^2 x\,dx\); the new limits are \(u=0\) and \(u=1\). Thus \(\int_0^1 u\,du=\left[\frac{u^2}{2}\right]_0^1=\frac{1}{2}\).

Model example 8.2. Evaluate \(\int x e^x\,dx\).

Choose \(u=x\) and \(dv=e^x\,dx\). Then \(du=dx\) and \(v=e^x\), so \(\int x e^x\,dx=xe^x-\int e^x\,dx=xe^x-e^x+C=e^x(x-1)+C\).

9. Applications of Integration

Area between curves. If \(f\) and \(g\) are continuous and \(f(x)\ge g(x)\) on \([a,b]\), the area is \(\int_a^b\big(f(x)-g(x)\big)\,dx\). Plot or test points to learn which curve is on top, and split the interval where the order changes.
Volumes. State the axis of rotation and require nonnegative radii.
  • Disk about the \(x\)-axis: \(V=\pi\int_a^b\big[f(x)\big]^2\,dx\) for the region under \(y=f(x)\).
  • Washer about the \(x\)-axis: \(V=\pi\int_a^b\left(\big[f(x)\big]^2-\big[g(x)\big]^2\right)\,dx\) for \(f(x)\ge g(x)\ge 0\).
  • Shell about the \(y\)-axis: \(V=2\pi\int_a^b x\,f(x)\,dx\) for the region under \(y=f(x)\) with \(0\le a \lt b\).
Work. A variable force \(F(x)\) acting from \(x=a\) to \(x=b\) does work \(W=\int_a^b F(x)\,dx\); for a spring, \(F(x)=kx\).
Model example 9.1 (area between \(y=x\) and \(y=x^3\)). Find the area enclosed by the two curves.

Intersections: \(x^3=x\) gives \(x(x^2-1)=0\), so \(x=-1,0,1\). On \([-1,0]\), test \(x=-\frac{1}{2}\): \(x^3=-\frac{1}{8} \gt -\frac{1}{2}=x\), so the top curve is \(y=x^3\). On \([0,1]\), test \(x=\frac{1}{2}\): \(x=\frac{1}{2} \gt \frac{1}{8}=x^3\), so the top curve is \(y=x\).

Therefore the area is the sum of one integral on each subinterval.

\[\int_{-1}^0(x^3-x)\,dx+\int_0^1(x-x^3)\,dx=\left[\frac{x^4}{4}-\frac{x^2}{2}\right]_{-1}^0+\left[\frac{x^2}{2}-\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1.\]

The first bracket evaluates to \(0-\left(\frac{1}{4}-\frac{1}{2}\right)=\frac{1}{4}\), and the second to \(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{4}\). Hence the total area is \(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{1}{4}=\frac{1}{2}\).

Model example 9.2 (washer). Rotate the region between \(y=x\) and \(y=x^2\) on \([0,1]\) about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume.

On \([0,1]\), \(x\ge x^2\ge 0\), so the outer radius is \(x\) and the inner radius is \(x^2\): \(V=\pi\int_0^1\left(x^2-x^4\right)dx=\pi\left[\frac{x^3}{3}-\frac{x^5}{5}\right]_0^1=\pi\left(\frac{1}{3}-\frac{1}{5}\right)=\frac{2\pi}{15}\).

Model example 9.3 (shell). Rotate the region under \(y=x^2\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=1\) about the \(y\)-axis. Find the volume.

A cylindrical shell at position \(x\) has radius \(x\), height \(x^2\), and thickness \(dx\), so \(V=2\pi\int_0^1 x\cdot x^2\,dx=2\pi\int_0^1 x^3\,dx=2\pi\left[\frac{x^4}{4}\right]_0^1=\frac{\pi}{2}\).

Model example 9.4 (work). A spring has constant \(20\) lb/ft. Find the work required to stretch it \(3\) ft from its natural length.

Hooke's law gives \(F(x)=20x\), so \(W=\int_0^3 20x\,dx=\left[10x^2\right]_0^3=90\) ft-lb.

10. Common Mistakes to Avoid

  • \(\lim_{x\to a}\frac{\sin x}{x}=1\) only when \(a=0\); as \(x\to\infty\) the limit is \(0\).
  • The chain rule is \((f\circ g)'(x)=f'(g(x))\,g'(x)\), not \(f'(x)g'(x)\) or \(f'(g(x))\) alone.
  • The power rule for integrals requires \(n\ne -1\); the case \(n=-1\) gives \(\ln|x|+C\).
  • Write \(\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx=\ln|x|+C\); the absolute value is necessary on negative \(x\).
  • Substitution is an equality: \(\int f(g(x))g'(x)\,dx=\int f(u)\,du=F(g(x))+C\). Do not drop \(g'(x)\) or forget to change definite-integral limits.
  • The MVT needs \(a \lt b\), continuity on \([a,b]\), and differentiability on \((a,b)\); verify all three.
  • FTC II needs \(f\) continuous on \([a,b]\), and FTC I needs \(f\) continuous at the evaluation point.
  • Differentiating a variable upper limit contributes an extra factor from the chain rule.
  • For area, test which curve is on top on each subinterval; the curves \(y=x\) and \(y=x^3\) swap order at \(0\).
  • State the axis of revolution and use outer radius minus inner radius for washers, never the sum.
  • In related rates, a negative \(\frac{dy}{dt}\) means decreasing; the speed is its absolute value.

11. Study and Partial-Credit Advice

  • Show the setup: write the defining equation, the substitution, or the integral before doing arithmetic. A correct setup usually earns substantial partial credit even if the final number is off.
  • Name the theorem and check its hypotheses: saying "by the MVT, since \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\) and differentiable on \((a,b)\)" demonstrates more understanding than quoting a formula.
  • Verify answers independently: differentiate an antiderivative, check units, and test whether a rate's sign matches the physical situation.
  • Follow your course's calculator policy: when calculators are permitted, use them to check arithmetic or approximate roots, not to replace an analytic setup that the question asks you to show.
  • Read the question: distinguish "rate of change" from "speed," "area" from "net signed integral," and "local" from "absolute" extrema.
  • Budget time: attempt every problem, write down the relevant formula even if stuck, and return to hard questions after securing the straightforward ones.

12. Mixed Practice Problems

  1. Evaluate \(\lim_{x\to 0}\frac{1-\cos x}{x^2}\).
  2. Differentiate \(y=\frac{\sin x}{x}\).
  3. Find \(y'\) if \(x^2+xy-y^2=4\).
  4. Find \(\frac{d}{dx}\int_{x}^{x^2}\sqrt{1+t^2}\,dt\).
  5. Evaluate \(\int_0^{\pi/4}\tan x\sec^2 x\,dx\).
  6. Evaluate \(\int \frac{x}{x^2+1}\,dx\).
  7. Evaluate \(\int_0^1 x e^{x^2}\,dx\).
  8. Find the area between \(y=x^2\) and \(y=x+2\).
  9. Rotate the region under \(y=x^2\) from \(x=0\) to \(x=2\) about the \(x\)-axis. Find the volume.
  10. A sphere's radius increases at \(1\) cm/s. Find \(\frac{dV}{dt}\) when \(r=2\) cm.
  11. A rectangle has perimeter \(24\) m. Find its maximum area.
  12. Evaluate \(\int_1^e\frac{1}{x}\,dx\).
  13. Evaluate \(\int \frac{1}{1+x^2}\,dx\).
  14. Verify the MVT for \(f(x)=x^3\) on \([-1,2]\) and find all valid \(c\).
  15. Evaluate \(\lim_{x\to\infty}\frac{e^x}{x^2}\).

Answer Key

  1. \(\frac{1}{2}\), using \(1-\cos x=2\sin^2(x/2)\) and \(\frac{\sin(x/2)}{x/2}\to 1\).
  2. \(y'=\frac{x\cos x-\sin x}{x^2}\), for \(x\ne 0\).
  3. \(y'=-\frac{2x+y}{x-2y}\), provided \(x-2y\ne 0\).
  4. \(2x\sqrt{1+x^4}-\sqrt{1+x^2}\).
  5. \(\frac{1}{2}\), with \(u=\tan x\).
  6. \(\frac{1}{2}\ln(x^2+1)+C\).
  7. \(\frac{1}{2}(e-1)\).
  8. \(\frac{9}{2}\), from \(\int_{-1}^2(x+2-x^2)\,dx\).
  9. \(\frac{32\pi}{5}\), from \(\pi\int_0^2 x^4\,dx\).
  10. \(16\pi\) cm\(^3\)/s, since \(\frac{dV}{dt}=4\pi r^2\frac{dr}{dt}=4\pi(4)(1)\).
  11. \(36\) m\(^2\), for a \(6\) m by \(6\) m square.
  12. \(1\).
  13. \(\arctan x+C\).
  14. Hypotheses hold; the secant slope is \(3\) and \(f'(c)=3c^2=3\) gives \(c=1\), which lies in \((-1,2)\). The root \(c=-1\) is an endpoint and is excluded.
  15. \(\infty\), because exponential growth dominates any power of \(x\).

13. Bonus: An Integration-Strategy Decision Tree

Work through these questions in order.
  1. Is it a definite integral? If so, note the limits and whether the integrand is continuous on the interval; handle discontinuities as improper integrals.
  2. Is it a basic form? Check the memorized table first, including \(\int x^n\,dx\) with \(n\ne -1\) and \(\int \frac{1}{x}\,dx=\ln|x|+C\).
  3. Can algebra simplify it? Expand products, split fractions, complete the square, or use a trig identity such as \(\sec^2 x=1+\tan^2 x\).
  4. Is there an inside function whose derivative is present? Try \(u\)-substitution: set \(u=g(x)\), compute \(du=g'(x)\,dx\), and rewrite the entire integral, including the limits if definite.
  5. Is it a product of two different families? Try integration by parts, \(\int u\,dv=uv-\int v\,du\), choosing \(u\) by the rule that logarithms and inverse trig functions come first, then polynomials, then trig and exponentials.
  6. Does it involve powers of trig functions? Save one factor for \(du\): use \(\sin^2 x+\cos^2 x=1\) for odd powers, and half-angle identities for even powers.
  7. Still stuck? Differentiate your candidate answer to see what integrand it produces, then adjust constants. On an exam, write the correct approach even if the antiderivative is incomplete.
Bonus model example. Decide a strategy for \(\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\,dx\).

The inner expression \(x^2+9\) has derivative \(2x\), which appears up to a constant, so substitution is the right choice. Let \(u=x^2+9\), \(du=2x\,dx\); then \(\int \frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\,dx=\frac{1}{2}\int u^{-1/2}\,du=\sqrt{u}+C=\sqrt{x^2+9}+C\). Differentiation confirms \(\frac{d}{dx}\sqrt{x^2+9}=\frac{x}{\sqrt{x^2+9}}\).
