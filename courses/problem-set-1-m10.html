Problem Set 1: Measurement

Mathematics 10 - Chapter 1 Medium

Problems

Problem 1

Convert \(48\) inches to feet and inches. How many yards is this?

Problem 2

Convert \(1.75\) meters to centimeters and millimeters.

Problem 3

A right pyramid has a square base with side length \(6\) m and slant height \(5\) m. Find its surface area using \(SA = B + \frac{1}{2}Pl\).

Problem 4

A right cone has radius \(4\) cm and slant height \(5\) cm. Find its volume. (Use \(V = \frac{1}{3}\pi r^2 h\) and find height first using Pythagorean theorem: \(l^2 = r^2 + h^2\))

Problem 5

A sphere has surface area \(100\pi\) cm². Find its radius and volume using \(SA = 4\pi r^2\) and \(V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\).

Problem 6

Convert \(2500\) cm to meters and centimeters. Then convert to feet using \(1\) foot \(= 30.48\) cm.

Problem 7

A rectangular prism has dimensions \(8\) ft × \(6\) ft × \(4\) ft. Find its total surface area and volume.

Problem 8

A cylinder has radius \(3\) m and height \(7\) m. Find its surface area \(SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh\) and volume \(V = \pi r^2 h\). Compare the volume to a cone with the same base and half the height.

Bonus Problems

Bonus Problem 1

Challenge: A cylindrical water tank with radius \(2\) m and height \(5\) m is filled to \(80\%\) capacity. A spherical ball with radius \(0.5\) m is submerged in the tank, displacing some water. Calculate the new water level (height) in the tank. Use \(V_{sphere} = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\) and assume the tank's cross-sectional area remains constant.

Bonus Problem 2

Challenge: A company needs to design a cylindrical can that holds exactly \(500\) cm³ of soup. The can must have a height-to-radius ratio of \(3:1\) (i.e., \(h = 3r\)). Find the dimensions (radius and height) of the can, then calculate its total surface area. Use \(V = \pi r^2 h\) and \(SA = 2\pi r^2 + 2\pi rh\).

Bonus Problem 3

Challenge: A right square pyramid and a cone have the same base area and the same slant height. The pyramid's base side length is \(6\) cm and its slant height is \(5\) cm. Find the radius of the cone's base, then compare the total surface areas of both shapes. Which has the larger surface area and by how much? Use \(SA_{pyramid} = B + \frac{1}{2}Pl\) and \(SA_{cone} = \pi r^2 + \pi rl\).
