Problem Set 3: Derivatives - SOLUTIONS

Calculus I - Week 3

Solution Guide

Note: These are detailed solutions showing all steps and reasoning. Each step is clearly justified.

Problem 1: Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = x^3 - 6x^2 + 5x - 2\)

Solution:
Step 1: Apply the power rule to each term
The power rule states: \(\frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1}\)
- \(\frac{d}{dx}[x^3] = 3x^2\)
- \(\frac{d}{dx}[-6x^2] = -6 \cdot 2x = -12x\)
- \(\frac{d}{dx}[5x] = 5\)
- \(\frac{d}{dx}[-2] = 0\) (derivative of constant)
Step 2: Combine the results
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 - 12x + 5\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx} = 3x^2 - 12x + 5}\)

Problem 2: Find equation of tangent line to \(y = x^2\) at \(x = 3\)

Solution:
Step 1: Find the point of tangency
\(y = 3^2 = 9\), so the point is \((3, 9)\).
Step 2: Find the slope using the derivative
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = 2x\)
At \(x = 3\): \(\frac{dy}{dx}\bigg|_{x=3} = 2(3) = 6\)
Step 3: Use point-slope form
The equation of a line with slope \(m\) passing through \((x_1, y_1)\) is:
\(y - y_1 = m(x - x_1)\)
Substituting: \(y - 9 = 6(x - 3)\)
Step 4: Simplify
\(y - 9 = 6x - 18\)
\(y = 6x - 9\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{y = 6x - 9}\)

Problem 3: Use definition to find \(f'(x)\) for \(f(x) = \frac{1}{x}\)

Solution:
Step 1: Write the definition of the derivative
\(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{f(x+h) - f(x)}{h}\)
Step 2: Substitute \(f(x+h)\) and \(f(x)\)
\(f(x+h) = \frac{1}{x+h}\) and \(f(x) = \frac{1}{x}\)
\(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\frac{1}{x+h} - \frac{1}{x}}{h}\)
Step 3: Combine fractions in the numerator
Common denominator: \(x(x+h)\)
\(\frac{1}{x+h} - \frac{1}{x} = \frac{x - (x+h)}{x(x+h)} = \frac{-h}{x(x+h)}\)
Step 4: Simplify the expression
\(f'(x) = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{\frac{-h}{x(x+h)}}{h} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{-h}{hx(x+h)} = \lim_{h \to 0} \frac{-1}{x(x+h)}\)
Step 5: Evaluate the limit
As \(h \to 0\): \(f'(x) = \frac{-1}{x \cdot x} = \frac{-1}{x^2}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{f'(x) = -\frac{1}{x^2}}\)

Problem 4: Find \(\frac{d}{dx}[e^{2x} \cdot \ln x]\)

Solution:
Step 1: Identify the product rule
For \(u \cdot v\), the derivative is: \((uv)' = u'v + uv'\)
Step 2: Identify \(u\) and \(v\)
Let \(u = e^{2x}\) and \(v = \ln x\)
Step 3: Find \(u'\) using chain rule
\(u = e^{2x}\)
Let \(w = 2x\), so \(u = e^w\)
\(\frac{du}{dx} = \frac{du}{dw} \cdot \frac{dw}{dx} = e^w \cdot 2 = 2e^{2x}\)
Step 4: Find \(v'\)
\(v = \ln x\)
\(v' = \frac{1}{x}\)
Step 5: Apply the product rule
\(\frac{d}{dx}[e^{2x} \cdot \ln x] = 2e^{2x} \cdot \ln x + e^{2x} \cdot \frac{1}{x}\)
Step 6: Factor and simplify
\(= e^{2x}\left(2\ln x + \frac{1}{x}\right)\)
\(= \frac{e^{2x}}{x}(2x\ln x + 1)\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{e^{2x}}{x}(2x\ln x + 1)}\)

Problem 5: Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = \sin(x^2 + 1)\)

Solution:
Step 1: Identify the chain rule structure
We have an outer function \(\sin(u)\) where \(u = x^2 + 1\).
Step 2: Apply the chain rule
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{du}[\sin u] \cdot \frac{du}{dx}\)
\(= \cos u \cdot \frac{d}{dx}[x^2 + 1]\)
Step 3: Compute the inner derivative
\(\frac{d}{dx}[x^2 + 1] = 2x\)
Step 4: Substitute back
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = \cos(x^2 + 1) \cdot 2x\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{2x\cos(x^2 + 1)}\)

Problem 6: Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = \frac{x^2 + 1}{x - 3}\)

Solution:
Step 1: Identify the quotient rule
For \(\frac{u}{v}\), the derivative is: \(\frac{u'v - uv'}{v^2}\)
Step 2: Identify \(u\) and \(v\)
\(u = x^2 + 1\) and \(v = x - 3\)
Step 3: Find derivatives
\(u' = 2x\) and \(v' = 1\)
Step 4: Apply the quotient rule
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{(2x)(x-3) - (x^2+1)(1)}{(x-3)^2}\)
Step 5: Expand the numerator
Numerator: \(2x(x-3) - (x^2+1) = 2x^2 - 6x - x^2 - 1 = x^2 - 6x - 1\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{x^2 - 6x - 1}{(x-3)^2}}\)

Problem 7: Position \(s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t\). Find velocity at \(t = 2\)

Solution:
Step 1: Understand the relationship
Velocity is the derivative of position: \(v(t) = s'(t)\)
Step 2: Find the velocity function
\(s(t) = t^3 - 6t^2 + 9t\)
\(v(t) = s'(t) = 3t^2 - 12t + 9\)
Step 3: Evaluate at t = 2
\(v(2) = 3(2)^2 - 12(2) + 9 = 12 - 24 + 9 = -3\)
Interpretation:
The negative velocity means the particle is moving in the negative direction at \(t = 2\).
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{v(2) = -3 \text{ units per time}}\)

Problem 8: Find critical numbers of \(f(x) = x^3 - 3x^2 - 9x + 5\)

Solution:
Step 1: Find the derivative
\(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 6x - 9\)
Step 2: Set derivative equal to zero
\(3x^2 - 6x - 9 = 0\)
Step 3: Simplify and factor
Divide by 3: \(x^2 - 2x - 3 = 0\)
Factor: \((x - 3)(x + 1) = 0\)
Step 4: Solve for x
\(x - 3 = 0 \Rightarrow x = 3\)
\(x + 1 = 0 \Rightarrow x = -1\)
Final Answer:
Critical numbers: \(\boxed{x = -1 \text{ and } x = 3}\)

Problem 9: Find \(\frac{d}{dx}[\tan^{-1}(x^2)]\)

Solution:
Step 1: Recall the derivative of arctangent
\(\frac{d}{dx}[\tan^{-1}(u)] = \frac{1}{1+u^2} \cdot \frac{du}{dx}\)
Step 2: Apply the chain rule
Here \(u = x^2\), so \(\frac{du}{dx} = 2x\)
\(\frac{d}{dx}[\tan^{-1}(x^2)] = \frac{1}{1+(x^2)^2} \cdot 2x\)
Step 3: Simplify
\(= \frac{2x}{1+x^4}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{2x}{1+x^4}}\)

Problem 10: Prove \(\frac{d}{dx}[x^n] = nx^{n-1}\) for \(n = \frac{1}{2}\)

Solution:
Step 1: Rewrite the function
\(f(x) = x^{1/2} = \sqrt{x}\)
Step 2: Apply the power rule
Using \(\frac{d}{dx}[x^r] = rx^{r-1}\):
\(f'(x) = \frac{1}{2}x^{1/2 - 1} = \frac{1}{2}x^{-1/2}\)
Step 3: Express with positive exponent
\(= \frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{1}{x^{1/2}} = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{f'(x) = \frac{1}{2\sqrt{x}}}\)

Bonus 1: Find \(\frac{dy}{dx}\) for \(y = (\sin x)^{\cos x}\)

Solution:
Step 1: Use logarithmic differentiation
Let \(y = (\sin x)^{\cos x}\). Take the natural logarithm of both sides:
Step 2: Apply logarithm
\(\ln y = \cos x \cdot \ln(\sin x)\)
Step 3: Differentiate both sides
\(\frac{1}{y} \cdot \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{d}{dx}[\cos x \cdot \ln(\sin x)]\)
Step 4: Apply product rule on the right
\(\frac{d}{dx}[\cos x \cdot \ln(\sin x)] = (-\sin x)\ln(\sin x) + \cos x \cdot \frac{1}{\sin x} \cdot \cos x\)
\(= -\sin x \ln(\sin x) + \cos^2 x \cdot \frac{1}{\sin x}\)
\(= -\sin x \ln(\sin x) + \cot x \cos x\)
Step 5: Solve for dy/dx
\(\frac{dy}{dx} = y[-\sin x \ln(\sin x) + \cot x \cos x]\)
\(= (\sin x)^{\cos x}[-\sin x \ln(\sin x) + \cot x \cos x]\)
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{\frac{dy}{dx} = (\sin x)^{\cos x}[-\sin x \ln(\sin x) + \cot x \cos x]}\)

Bonus 2: If \(f(g(x)) = x\) for all \(x\), prove that \(g(f(x)) = x\) for all \(x\) in the appropriate domains

Solution:
Step 1: Understand what \(f(g(x)) = x\) means
This says that \(g\) is a left inverse of \(f\), meaning applying \(g\) after \(f\) (in that order) returns the original input.
Step 2: Differentiate both sides
Using the chain rule: \(\frac{d}{dx}[f(g(x))] = f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) = \frac{d}{dx}[x] = 1\)
Step 3: Conclude non-zero derivative
Since \(f'(g(x)) \cdot g'(x) = 1\), neither \(f'(g(x))\) nor \(g'(x)\) can be zero.
Step 4: Apply the Inverse Function Theorem
If \(f\) is differentiable and \(f'(g(x)) \neq 0\), then \(f\) has a local inverse near each point.
Since \(g\) is a local inverse of \(f\) and inverses are unique, \(g(f(x)) = x\) on the range of \(f\).
Final Answer:
\(\boxed{g(f(x)) = x \text{ for all } x \text{ in the appropriate domain}}\)
