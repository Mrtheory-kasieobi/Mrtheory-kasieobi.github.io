Linear and Quadratic Inequalities

Precalculus 11 - Lecture Notes
British Columbia Curriculum | Last Updated: 2026

1. Introduction to Inequalities

An inequality is a mathematical statement that compares two expressions using inequality symbols: \(<\), \(>\), \(\leq\), or \(\geq\).

Definition: A linear inequality in one variable can be written in the form \(ax + b < c\), \(ax + b > c\), \(ax + b \leq c\), or \(ax + b \geq c\), where \(a \neq 0\).

1.1 Inequality Symbols

  • \(<\) : less than
  • \(>\) : greater than
  • \(\leq\) : less than or equal to
  • \(\geq\) : greater than or equal to

2. Linear Inequalities in One Variable

Solving linear inequalities is similar to solving linear equations, with one important exception: when multiplying or dividing by a negative number, reverse the inequality sign.

Important Rule:

When you multiply or divide both sides of an inequality by a negative number, you must reverse the inequality sign.

2.1 Solving Linear Inequalities

Example 1: Basic Linear Inequality

Solve: \(3x - 5 < 10\)

Solution:

\(3x < 15\)

\(x < 5\)

Solution set: \(\{x \mid x < 5\}\) or \((-\infty, 5)\) in interval notation

Example 2: Inequality with Negative Coefficient

Solve: \(-2x + 3 \geq 7\)

Solution:

\(-2x \geq 4\)

Divide by -2 (reverse sign): \(x \leq -2\)

Solution set: \(\{x \mid x \leq -2\}\) or \((-\infty, -2]\) in interval notation

2.2 Interval Notation

Interval notation provides a concise way to express solution sets:

  • Parentheses \(( )\) indicate the endpoint is not included
  • Brackets \([ ]\) indicate the endpoint is included
  • \(-\infty\) and \(\infty\) always use parentheses

2.3 Graphing Linear Inequalities

To graph a linear inequality on a number line:

  1. Solve the inequality
  2. Use an open circle for \(<\) or \(>\)
  3. Use a closed circle for \(\leq\) or \(\geq\)
  4. Shade in the appropriate direction

3. Linear Inequalities in Two Variables

A linear inequality in two variables has the form \(ax + by < c\), \(ax + by > c\), \(ax + by \leq c\), or \(ax + by \geq c\).

3.1 Graphing Linear Inequalities

To graph a linear inequality in two variables:

  1. Graph the boundary line (dashed for \(<\) or \(>\), solid for \(\leq\) or \(\geq\))
  2. Choose a test point not on the line
  3. Shade the region that satisfies the inequality
Example 3: Graphing Linear Inequalities

Graph: \(y < 2x + 1\)

Solution:

Step 1: Graph the line \(y = 2x + 1\) with a dashed line (since \(<\))

Step 2: Test point \((0, 0)\): \(0 < 2(0) + 1\) → \(0 < 1\) ✓

Step 3: Shade the region below the line

4. Systems of Linear Inequalities

A system of linear inequalities consists of two or more inequalities. The solution is the region where all inequalities overlap.

Example 4: System of Linear Inequalities

Solve the system: \(\begin{cases} y > x - 2 \\ y \leq -x + 4 \end{cases}\)

Solution:

Graph both inequalities and find the overlapping region.

The solution is the region between the two lines.

5. Quadratic Inequalities in One Variable

A quadratic inequality involves a quadratic expression. To solve, find where the quadratic is positive or negative.

5.1 Solving Quadratic Inequalities

To solve a quadratic inequality:

  1. Rewrite in standard form: \(ax^2 + bx + c < 0\) or \(ax^2 + bx + c > 0\)
  2. Find the roots of the corresponding equation
  3. Test intervals determined by the roots
  4. Identify where the inequality is satisfied
Example 5: Quadratic Inequality

Solve: \(x^2 - 5x + 6 > 0\)

Solution:

Step 1: Find roots: \(x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0\)

\((x - 2)(x - 3) = 0\)

Roots: \(x = 2\) and \(x = 3\)

Step 2: Test intervals: \((-\infty, 2)\), \((2, 3)\), \((3, \infty)\)

Test \(x = 0\): \(0^2 - 5(0) + 6 = 6 > 0\) ✓

Test \(x = 2.5\): \((2.5)^2 - 5(2.5) + 6 = 6.25 - 12.5 + 6 = -0.25 < 0\) ✗

Test \(x = 4\): \(4^2 - 5(4) + 6 = 16 - 20 + 6 = 2 > 0\) ✓

Solution: \(x < 2\) or \(x > 3\), or \((-\infty, 2) \cup (3, \infty)\)

5.2 Sign Chart Method

A sign chart helps visualize where a quadratic is positive or negative:

Sign Chart for \(x^2 - 5x + 6\):
Interval: \((-\infty, 2)\) | \((2, 3)\) | \((3, \infty)\)
Sign: \(+\) | \(-\) | \(+\)

6. Quadratic Inequalities in Two Variables

Quadratic inequalities in two variables involve regions bounded by parabolas.

Example 6: Quadratic Inequality in Two Variables

Graph: \(y \leq x^2 - 4\)

Solution:

Step 1: Graph the parabola \(y = x^2 - 4\) with a solid line

Step 2: Test point \((0, 0)\): \(0 \leq 0^2 - 4\) → \(0 \leq -4\) ✗

Step 3: Shade the region outside the parabola

7. Summary of Key Concepts

Concept Key Points
Linear Inequality (1 var) Solve like equation, reverse sign when multiplying/dividing by negative
Interval Notation \((a, b)\) exclusive, \([a, b]\) inclusive
Linear Inequality (2 var) Graph boundary line, test point, shade region
Quadratic Inequality (1 var) Find roots, test intervals, determine sign
Quadratic Inequality (2 var) Graph parabola, test point, shade region
