Radical Expressions and Equations

Precalculus 11 - Lecture Notes
British Columbia Curriculum | Last Updated: 2026

1. Introduction to Radicals

A radical expression is an expression that contains a square root, cube root, or any other root. The most common radical is the square root.

Definition: The square root of a number \(x\) is a number \(y\) such that \(y^2 = x\). It is written as \(\sqrt{x}\) or \(x^{1/2}\).

1.1 Radical Notation

The general form of a radical is:

\[\sqrt[n]{a} = a^{1/n}\]

Where \(n\) is the index of the radical and \(a\) is the radicand.

1.2 Properties of Radicals

  • \(\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b}\) (product property)
  • \(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\) (quotient property)
  • \(\sqrt{a^2} = |a|\) (square root of a square)

2. Working with Radicals

2.1 Simplifying Radicals

To simplify a radical, factor the radicand into perfect squares:

Example 1: Simplifying Radicals

Simplify \(\sqrt{72}\)

Solution:

Factor 72: \(72 = 36 \times 2 = 6^2 \times 2\)

\[\sqrt{72} = \sqrt{36 \times 2} = \sqrt{36} \cdot \sqrt{2} = 6\sqrt{2}\]

2.2 Rationalizing the Denominator

To rationalize a denominator containing a radical, multiply numerator and denominator by the radical:

Example 2: Rationalizing the Denominator

Simplify \(\frac{3}{\sqrt{5}}\)

Solution:

\[\frac{3}{\sqrt{5}} \cdot \frac{\sqrt{5}}{\sqrt{5}} = \frac{3\sqrt{5}}{5}\]

3. Multiplying and Dividing Radical Expressions

3.1 Multiplying Radicals

Use the product property: \(\sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b} = \sqrt{ab}\)

Example 3: Multiplying Radicals

Simplify: \(\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{12}\)

Solution:

\[\sqrt{3} \cdot \sqrt{12} = \sqrt{3 \times 12} = \sqrt{36} = 6\]

3.2 Dividing Radicals

Use the quotient property: \(\frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}} = \sqrt{\frac{a}{b}}\)

Example 4: Dividing Radicals

Simplify: \(\frac{\sqrt{50}}{\sqrt{2}}\)

Solution:

\[\frac{\sqrt{50}}{\sqrt{2}} = \sqrt{\frac{50}{2}} = \sqrt{25} = 5\]

3.3 Multiplying Binomials with Radicals

Use FOIL method and combine like terms:

Example 5: Multiplying Binomials

Expand: \((2 + \sqrt{3})(3 - \sqrt{3})\)

Solution:

\[= 6 - 2\sqrt{3} + 3\sqrt{3} - 3\]

\[= 3 + \sqrt{3}\]

4. Radical Equations

A radical equation is an equation that contains a radical expression with the variable in the radicand.

Definition: A radical equation is an equation in which the variable appears under a radical sign.

4.1 Solving Radical Equations

To solve a radical equation:

  1. Isolate the radical on one side
  2. Square both sides (or cube, etc.)
  3. Solve the resulting equation
  4. Check all solutions in the original equation (extraneous solutions may occur)
Example 6: Solving a Radical Equation

Solve: \(\sqrt{x + 3} = x - 1\)

Solution:

Step 1: Square both sides:

\[x + 3 = (x - 1)^2\]

Step 2: Expand and simplify:

\[x + 3 = x^2 - 2x + 1\]

\[0 = x^2 - 3x - 2\]

Step 3: Factor: \((x - 1)(x - 2) = 0\)

Step 4: Check solutions: \(x = 1\) or \(x = 2\)

Check \(x = 1\): \(\sqrt{1 + 3} = 0\) → \(2 \neq 0\) (extraneous)

Check \(x = 2\): \(\sqrt{2 + 3} = 1\) → \(\sqrt{5} \neq 1\) (extraneous)

No solution

4.2 Equations with Multiple Radicals

When an equation has multiple radicals, isolate one radical at a time:

Example 7: Multiple Radicals

Solve: \(\sqrt{x + 2} + \sqrt{x - 1} = 3\)

Solution:

Step 1: Isolate one radical: \(\sqrt{x + 2} = 3 - \sqrt{x - 1}\)

Step 2: Square both sides and simplify

Step 3: Isolate the remaining radical and square again

Step 4: Solve the resulting quadratic equation

Step 5: Check all solutions

5. Summary of Key Concepts

Concept Formula/Definition
Radical Notation \(\sqrt[n]{a} = a^{1/n}\)
Product Property \(\sqrt{ab} = \sqrt{a} \cdot \sqrt{b}\)
Quotient Property \(\sqrt{\frac{a}{b}} = \frac{\sqrt{a}}{\sqrt{b}}\)
Rationalizing Multiply by conjugate or same radical
Solving Radical Equations Isolate, square, solve, check
