Problem Set 13: Irrational Numbers and Approximation

Elementary Number Theory - Week 14 Medium

Problems

Problem 1

Prove that \(\sqrt{5}\) is irrational using the method of infinite descent.

Problem 2

Show that \(\log_2 3\) is irrational.

Problem 3

Prove: if \(\alpha\) is irrational, then \(\alpha^2\) may be either rational or irrational. Give examples of each.

Problem 4

Apply Dirichlet's theorem to find a rational approximation to \(\sqrt{2}\) with denominator at most 100.

Problem 5

Show that for any rational \(\frac{p}{q}\) in lowest terms, \(\left|\sqrt{2} - \frac{p}{q}\right| > \frac{1}{3q^2}\).

Problem 6

Prove that Liouville numbers are transcendental using the definition directly.

Problem 7

Find the continued fraction expansion of \(\sqrt{19}\) and use it to approximate \(\sqrt{19}\) to within 0.01.

Problem 8

Show that the set of irrational numbers is uncountable by constructing a bijection with the power set of natural numbers.

Problem 9

Prove: If \(\alpha\) is irrational, the set \(\{\{n\alpha\} : n \in \mathbb{Z}^+\}\) is dense in \([0, 1]\).

Problem 10

Show that for any irrational \(\alpha\) and any \(\epsilon > 0\), there exist integers \(p, q\) with \(q > 0\) such that \(\left|\alpha - \frac{p}{q}\right| < \epsilon\).

Bonus Problems

Bonus Problem 1

Challenge: Prove that \(e\) is irrational using the series \(e = \sum_{n=0}^{\infty} \frac{1}{n!}\).

Bonus Problem 2

Challenge: Show that at least one of \(\alpha\) and \(1-\alpha\) is irrational.

Bonus Problem 3

Challenge: Prove that \(\sqrt[3]{2}\) is irrational.
