Problem Set 1: Propositional Logic and Truth Tables

Introduction to Proofs - Lecture 1 Medium

Try each problem before reading the solutions. A proof is judged by the completeness of its logical chain, not by how short it is. State your assumptions, justify every nontrivial step, and verify that the conclusion follows exactly.

Problems

Problem 1

Determine whether each is a proposition, and if so give its truth value: (a) \(11\) is prime; (b) \(x+1=4\); (c) every even integer greater than \(2\) is a sum of two primes; (d) "watch out!".

Problem 2

Construct a truth table for \((p o q)\lor(p o r)\) and decide whether it is a tautology.

Problem 3

Prove \( eg(p\lor q)\equiv eg p\land eg q\) by truth tables.

Problem 4

Show that \((p o q)\equiv( eg q o eg p)\) without writing a truth table.

Problem 5

Prove or disprove: \((p o q)\lor(q o p)\) is a tautology.

Problem 6

Translate into logical symbols and state the converse: "If \(n\) is divisible by \(6\), then \(n\) is divisible by \(2\) and by \(3\)."

Problem 7

Analyse the argument: "If the series converges absolutely, it converges. This series converges. Therefore it converges absolutely." Name the form and determine validity.

Problem 8

Determine whether \( eg\big(p\land(q o eg p)\big)\) is a tautology, a contradiction, or contingent.

Bonus Problem

Determine whether the following argument is valid: “If \(n\) is divisible by \(6\), then \(n\) is even. The integer \(14\) is even. Therefore \(14\) is divisible by \(6\).”
