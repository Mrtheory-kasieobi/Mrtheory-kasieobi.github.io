Solutions: Problem Set 15: Pythagorean Triples

Solutions

Problem 1

Solution: For \(c = 25\): \(m^2+n^2 = 25\) gives \(m=4, n=3\). Triple: \((m^2-n^2, 2mn, m^2+n^2) = (7, 24, 25)\).

Problem 2

Solution: Primitive triples with \(c < 50\): \((3,4,5), (5,12,13), (8,15,17), (7,24,25), (20,21,29), (12,35,37), (9,40,41)\).

Problem 3

Solution: Modulo 3: squares are 0, 1. If neither leg is divisible by 3, \(a^2+b^2 \equiv 1+1 \equiv 2 \pmod{3}\), not a square.

Problem 4

Solution: Modulo 4: squares are 0, 1. If neither leg is even, \(a^2+b^2 \equiv 1+1 \equiv 2 \pmod{4}\), not a square.

Problem 5

Solution: In primitive triple, one leg divisible by 3, one by 4, one by 5. So \(abc\) divisible by \(3 \times 4 \times 5 = 60\).

Problem 6

Solution: For primitive triple \(a = m^2-n^2, b = 2mn, c = m^2+n^2\) with \(m,n\) of opposite parity. Then \(c \equiv 1+0 \equiv 1 \pmod{4}\).

Problem 7

Solution: Triangle \((3, 4, 5)\) has area \(\frac{3 \times 4}{2} = 6\). So 6 is a congruent number.

Problem 8

Solution: If \(n\) is congruent, triangle with sides \((ka, kb, kc)\) has area \(k^2 n\). For \(k=2\), \(2^2 \cdot 6 = 24\) is also congruent.

Problem 9

Solution: \(x^2+y^2=50\). Solutions: \((1, 7), (5, 5), (7, 1)\)

Problem 10

Solution: Triangle \((\frac{3}{2}, \frac{20}{3}, \frac{41}{6})\) has area \(\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{20}{3} = 5\).

Bonus Problems

Bonus Problem 1

Solution:

Step 1: A Fermat right triangle has integer sides \(a, b, c\) with area \(ab/2\) an integer.

Step 2: Such a triangle corresponds to a solution of \(x^2 - y^2 = z^2\) with \(xyz\) a square.

Step 3: This is related to the equation \(x^4 - y^4 = z^2\), which has no non-trivial solutions by infinite descent.

Bonus Problem 2

Solution:

Step 1: 5 is congruent if there exists a rational right triangle with area 5.

Step 2: Try triangle with sides \((\frac{3}{2}, \frac{20}{3}, \frac{41}{6})\).

Step 3: Area = \(\frac{1}{2} \cdot \frac{3}{2} \cdot \frac{20}{3} = 5\). ✓

Bonus Problem 3

Solution:

Step 1: If \(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\) is prime and \(p\) is congruent, there exists rational right triangle with area \(p\).

Step 2: Such a triangle corresponds to an elliptic curve \(y^2 = x^3 - p^2 x\).

Step 3: By 2-descent, this curve has rank 0 for \(p \equiv 3 \pmod{4}\), so only trivial points exist. No congruent number.
