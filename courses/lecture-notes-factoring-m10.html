Lecture 3: Factors and Products

Mathematics 10 - Chapter 3

3.1 Factors and Multiples of Whole Numbers

A factor of a number divides that number evenly. A multiple is the product of a number and an integer. Understanding factors and multiples is fundamental to number theory.

Why This Matters: Factors and multiples are the building blocks of arithmetic. They help us understand divisibility, simplify fractions, and solve many real-world problems. For example, if you want to arrange chairs in equal rows, you need to find factors of the total number of chairs.

Prime Factorization

Every positive integer greater than 1 can be expressed uniquely as a product of prime numbers.

Example: \(60 = 2^2 \times 3 \times 5\)

Application: This is used in cryptography to create secure codes.

Example 1: Finding All Factors

Find all factors of 36.

Using prime factorization: \(36 = 2^2 \times 3^2\)

Factors: 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 9, 12, 18, 36

Motivation: This helps in organizing groups, arranging objects, and solving many practical problems.

3.2 Factoring Polynomials

Polynomials can be factored using techniques similar to factoring numbers. The goal is to express a polynomial as a product of simpler polynomials.

Why Factor Polynomials? Factoring helps us solve equations, simplify expressions, and understand the behavior of functions. When we factor a quadratic, we can find its zeros, which tell us where the graph crosses the x-axis.

Common Factoring Patterns

Common Factor: \(ax + ay = a(x + y)\)

Difference of Squares: \(a^2 - b^2 = (a-b)(a+b)\)

Trinomial (a=1): \(x^2 + bx + c = (x + m)(x + n)\) where \(m \times n = c\) and \(m + n = b\)

Example 2: Factoring a Trinomial

Factor \(x^2 + 7x + 12\).

We need two numbers that multiply to 12 and add to 7: 3 and 4.

\(x^2 + 7x + 12 = (x + 3)(x + 4)\)

Motivation: This allows us to solve \(x^2 + 7x + 12 = 0\) by setting each factor to zero: \(x = -3\) or \(x = -4\).

3.3 Factoring Quadratic Expressions

Quadratic expressions of the form \(ax^2 + bx + c\) can be factored when we can find two numbers that multiply to \(ac\) and add to \(b\).

Real-World Application: Projectile motion follows a parabolic path described by a quadratic equation. Factoring helps us find when the projectile hits the ground.

AC Method for Factoring

To factor \(ax^2 + bx + c\) where \(a \neq 1\):

  1. Find two numbers that multiply to \(ac\) and add to \(b\)
  2. Split the middle term using these numbers
  3. Factor by grouping

3.4 Math Lab: Factoring with Algebra Tiles

Algebra tiles provide a visual way to understand factoring. Each tile represents a different term, and arranging them helps us see the factors.

Connection to Geometry: The area model connects factoring to finding the area of rectangles. If we can arrange tiles into a rectangle, the dimensions give us the factors.

3.5 Powers and Products in Factoring

When factoring expressions with powers, we use the same principles but must be careful with exponents.

Key Rules:

  • \(a^m \times a^n = a^{m+n}\)
  • \(a^m \div a^n = a^{m-n}\)
  • \((a^m)^n = a^{mn}\)
Example 3: Factoring with Powers

Factor \(2x^3 + 4x^2 - 6x\).

Common factor: \(2x(x^2 + 2x - 3)\)

Factor the quadratic: \(2x(x + 3)(x - 1)\)

Motivation: This helps us understand the behavior of polynomial functions and find their zeros.

3.6 Factoring with Rational Coefficients

When coefficients are fractions, we can multiply by the LCD to make factoring easier, then divide by the LCD at the end.

Why This Technique Works: Multiplying by a constant doesn't change the roots of the equation, but it makes the arithmetic simpler.

3.7 Factoring Special Cases

Some special cases make factoring easier:

  • Perfect Square Trinomials: \(a^2 + 2ab + b^2 = (a + b)^2\)
  • Difference of Squares: \(a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)\)
  • Sum of Squares: Does not factor over real numbers
Example 4: Perfect Square Trinomial

Factor \(9x^2 + 24x + 16\).

Notice: \(9x^2 = (3x)^2\), \(16 = 4^2\), and \(24x = 2(3x)(4)\)

\(9x^2 + 24x + 16 = (3x + 4)^2\)

Motivation: This pattern appears in completing the square, a key technique for solving quadratic equations and finding vertex form.
