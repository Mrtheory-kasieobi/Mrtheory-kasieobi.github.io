Problem Set 10: Applications of Gaussian Integers

Elementary Number Theory - Week 11 Medium

Problems

Problem 1

Express 17 as a sum of two squares.

Problem 2

Show that 61 can be expressed as a sum of two squares and find the representation.

Problem 3

Solve \(x^2+y^2=89\) for integers \(x,y\).

Problem 4

Prove that a prime \(p\) can be written as \(x^2+y^2\) iff \(p=2\) or \(p\equiv 1\pmod4\).

Problem 5

Find the number of representations of 50 as a sum of two squares, counting signs and order.

Problem 6

Show that if \(p\equiv 3\pmod4\) is prime, then \(x^2+y^2=p\) has no integer solutions.

Problem 7

Find integer solutions to \(x^2+2y^2=17\).

Problem 8

Prove that if \(p\equiv 1\pmod4\) is prime, then \(p\mid x^2+1\) for some integer \(x\).

Problem 9

If \(m\) and \(n\) are both sums of two squares, show that \(mn\) is also a sum of two squares.

Problem 10

Use Gaussian integers to find a solution to \(x^2+y^2=125\).

Bonus Problems

Bonus Problem 1

Challenge: Find the number of ways to write 25 as a sum of two squares, counting signs and order.

Bonus Problem 2

Challenge: Prove: if every prime \(p\equiv 1\pmod4\) can be written as \(x^2+y^2\), then every integer whose prime factors \(q\equiv 3\pmod4\) all appear to even power can be written as \(x^2+y^2\).

Bonus Problem 3

Challenge: Find all integer solutions to \(x^2+5y^2=41\).
