Lecture 7: Quadratic Reciprocity Law

Lecture 7Quadratic Reciprocity Law

1. Statement of Quadratic Reciprocity

Definition 7.1 For an odd prime \(p\), define \(\epsilon(p) = (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\). Note that: \[\epsilon(p) = \begin{cases} 1 & \text{if } p \equiv 1 \pmod{4} \\ -1 & \text{if } p \equiv 3 \pmod{4} \end{cases}\]
Theorem 7.1 (Quadratic Reciprocity) Let \(p\) and \(q\) be distinct odd primes. Then: \[\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = \epsilon(q) \cdot \epsilon(p) \cdot \left(\frac{q}{p}\right)\] Equivalently: \[\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) \left(\frac{q}{p}\right) = (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2} \cdot \frac{q-1}{2}}\]

2. Gauss's Lemma

Theorem 7.2 (Gauss's Lemma) Let \(p\) be an odd prime and \(a\) an integer with \(\gcd(a, p) = 1\). Define the set: \[S = \left\{\frac{ap}{p}, \frac{2ap}{p}, \ldots, \frac{(p-1)ap}{p}\right\}\] Let \(\mu\) be the number of elements in \(S\) whose least positive residue is greater than \(\frac{p}{2}\). Then: \[\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) = (-1)^\mu\]
Proof:
Step 1: Set up the residues

Consider the residues \(r_1, r_2, \ldots, r_{p-1}\) where \(r_i\) is the least positive residue of \(ia\) modulo \(p\).

Since \(\gcd(a, p) = 1\), the residues \(\{r_1, r_2, \ldots, r_{p-1}\} = \{1, 2, \ldots, p-1\}\).

Step 2: Define the counting parameter

Define \(\mu = |\{i : r_i > \frac{p}{2}\}|\). For each such \(i\), let \(r_i' = p - r_i < \frac{p}{2}\).

Step 3: Consider the product of all residues

Consider the product: \[\prod_{i=1}^{p-1} r_i \equiv \prod_{i=1}^{p-1} ia = a^{p-1} \cdot (p-1)! \pmod{p}\]

Step 4: Apply Fermat's Little Theorem

By Fermat's Little Theorem, \(a^{p-1} \equiv 1 \pmod{p}\), so: \[\prod_{i=1}^{p-1} r_i \equiv (p-1)! \pmod{p}\]

Step 5: Partition the residues

Now partition the residues: \[\prod_{i=1}^{p-1} r_i = \left(\prod_{r_i < p/2} r_i\right) \cdot \left(\prod_{r_i > p/2} r_i\right)\]

Step 6: Transform large residues

For \(r_i > \frac{p}{2}\), we have \(r_i = p - r_i'\). Thus: \[\prod_{r_i > p/2} r_i = \prod_{r_i > p/2} (p - r_i') \equiv \prod_{r_i > p/2} (-r_i') \pmod{p}\] \[= (-1)^\mu \prod_{r_i > p/2} r_i'\]

Step 7: Combine and conclude

Therefore: \[\prod_{i=1}^{p-1} r_i \equiv (-1)^\mu \left(\prod_{r_i < p/2} r_i\right) \cdot \left(\prod_{r_i > p/2} r_i'\right) \pmod{p}\]

Since the sets \(\{r_i : r_i < p/2\}\) and \(\{r_i' : r_i > p/2\}\) partition \(\{1, 2, \ldots, \frac{p-1}{2}\}\): \[\prod_{i=1}^{p-1} r_i \equiv (-1)^\mu \cdot \left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)! \pmod{p}\]

Comparing with \(\prod_{i=1}^{p-1} r_i \equiv (p-1)! \pmod{p}\) and using \((p-1)! = (-1)^\mu \cdot \left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)! \cdot \left(\frac{p-1}{2}\right)!\): We get \(\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) = (-1)^\mu\). \(\square\)

3. Proof of Quadratic Reciprocity

Proof (Gauss's Lemma approach):
Step 1: Set up the geometric interpretation

We prove that \(\mu\left(\frac{q}{p}\right) + \mu\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) \equiv \frac{p-1}{2} \cdot \frac{q-1}{2} \pmod{2}\).

Consider the rectangle \(R = \{(x, y) \in \mathbb{R}^2 : 0 < x < \frac{p}{2}, 0 < y < \frac{q}{2}\}\).

The number of lattice points in \(R\) is \(\frac{p-1}{2} \cdot \frac{q-1}{2}\).

Step 2: Count lattice points below the line

Consider the line \(L: y = \frac{q}{p}x\). Count lattice points below this line in the rectangle.

This count equals \(\mu\left(\frac{q}{p}\right)\) by Gauss's Lemma.

Step 3: Count lattice points to the left

Similarly, counting points to the left of the line \(x = \frac{p}{q}y\) gives \(\mu\left(\frac{p}{q}\right)\).

Step 4: Apply symmetry

By symmetry, the total count satisfies: \[\mu\left(\frac{q}{p}\right) + \mu\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = \frac{p-1}{2} \cdot \frac{q-1}{2}\]

(The line passes through no lattice points with coordinates in the specified ranges.)

Step 5: Conclude

Therefore: \[\left(\frac{q}{p}\right) = (-1)^{\mu(q/p)} \quad \text{and} \quad \left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = (-1)^{\mu(p/q)}\] \[\left(\frac{q}{p}\right) \left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = (-1)^{\mu(q/p) + \mu(p/q)} = (-1)^{\frac{p-1}{2} \cdot \frac{q-1}{2}}\]

Thus \(\left(\frac{p}{q}\right) = \epsilon(p)\epsilon(q) \left(\frac{q}{p}\right)\), which is Quadratic Reciprocity. \(\square\)

Exercises:
  1. Compute \(\left(\frac{3}{11}\right)\) using Quadratic Reciprocity.
  2. Compute \(\left(\frac{5}{13}\right)\) using Quadratic Reciprocity.
  3. Prove: If \(p \equiv 1 \pmod{4}\), then \(\left(\frac{-1}{p}\right) = 1\).
  4. Show that \(\left(\frac{2}{p}\right) = 1\) iff \(p \equiv \pm 1 \pmod{8}\).
  5. Find all \(x\) such that \(x^2 \equiv 17 \pmod{29}\).
  6. Compute \(\left(\frac{11}{43}\right)\).
  7. Prove: \(\left(\frac{a}{p}\right) = 1\) iff \(x^2 \equiv a \pmod{p}\) has a solution.
