Elementary Number Theory

An elementary introduction to number theory with no algebraic prerequisites. Covers primes, congruences, quadratic reciprocity, diophantine equations, Gaussian integers, irrational numbers, continued fractions, and partitions. Based on MIT 18.781 Theory of Numbers.

Level: Undergraduate Prerequisite: None Last Updated: July 2026

Course Information

  • Level Undergraduate (Spring)
  • Estimated Duration 15 weeks
  • Prerequisites None
  • Recommended Textbooks Elementary Number Theory by David Burton; An Introduction to the Theory of Numbers by Niven, Zuckerman, and Montgomery
  • Software None required

Course Description

This course provides a rigorous introduction to elementary number theory. We begin with fundamental concepts of divisibility, primes, and the fundamental theorem of arithmetic. The course then develops the theory of congruences, including linear and quadratic congruences, and culminates with quadratic reciprocity.

A key addition to this course is the study of Gaussian integers, which extends the unique factorization properties of the integers to the complex plane. We explore arithmetic in Z[i], prime factorization in Gaussian integers, and applications to solving Diophantine equations.

Additional topics include Diophantine equations, irrational numbers and their approximations, continued fractions, and partitions. Each topic is presented with careful proofs and numerous examples.
