Related Rates and Optimization

Calculus I - Week 7

1. Related Rates

1.1 The Related Rates Method

Related Rates: A technique for finding the rate of change of one quantity in terms of the rate of change of another related quantity using implicit differentiation.
General Strategy:
  1. Identify the given rate(s) and the rate to find
  2. Write an equation relating the variables
  3. Differentiate both sides with respect to time \(t\)
  4. Substitute known values and solve for the unknown rate

1.2 Fundamental Theorem of Related Rates

Theorem 1.1: If quantities \(x\) and \(y\) are both functions of time \(t\) and are related by an equation \(F(x, y) = 0\), then at any time \(t_0\) where \(\frac{dx}{dt}\) and \(\frac{dy}{dt}\) exist: $$\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{\partial F/\partial x}{\partial F/\partial y} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}$$ provided \(\frac{\partial F}{\partial y} \neq 0\).
Proof: Given \(F(x, y) = 0\) where both \(x\) and \(y\) depend on \(t\). Differentiating implicitly with respect to \(t\): $$\frac{d}{dt}[F(x, y)] = \frac{\partial F}{\partial x}\frac{dx}{dt} + \frac{\partial F}{\partial y}\frac{dy}{dt} = 0$$ Solving for \(\frac{dy}{dt}\): $$\frac{dy}{dt} = -\frac{\partial F/\partial x}{\partial F/\partial y} \cdot \frac{dx}{dt}$$ ∎
Example 1.1: A spherical balloon is being inflated at a rate of 10 cm³/s. How fast is the radius increasing when the diameter is 50 cm?

Given: \(\frac{dV}{dt} = 10\) cm³/s. Find: \(\frac{dr}{dt}\) when \(r = 25\) cm.

Volume of sphere: \(V = \frac{4}{3}\pi r^3\)

Differentiate: \(\frac{dV}{dt} = 4\pi r^2 \frac{dr}{dt}\)

Substitute: \(10 = 4\pi (25)^2 \frac{dr}{dt}\)

Result: \(\frac{dr}{dt} = \frac{10}{2500\pi} = \frac{1}{250\pi}\) cm/s

2. Optimization

2.1 The Extreme Value Theorem

Theorem 2.1 (Extreme Value Theorem): If \(f\) is continuous on a closed interval \([a, b]\), then \(f\) attains both an absolute maximum and an absolute minimum on \([a, b]\).
Proof (Sketch): This is a fundamental result in real analysis. The proof relies on the completeness of the real numbers: 1. Since \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), the image \(f([a,b])\) is bounded (by the Bolzano-Weierstrass theorem). 2. Let \(M = \sup f([a,b])\). There exists a sequence \(\{x_n\}\) in \([a,b]\) with \(f(x_n) \to M\). 3. By Bolzano-Weierstrass, \(\{x_n\}\) has a convergent subsequence \(\{x_{n_k}\}\) with limit \(c \in [a,b]\). 4. By continuity, \(f(c) = \lim f(x_{n_k}) = M\). Hence the maximum is attained. A similar argument shows the minimum is attained. ∎

2.2 Critical Points and Fermat's Theorem

Definition 2.1 (Critical Point): A number \(c\) in the domain of \(f\) is a critical number of \(f\) if either \(f'(c) = 0\) or \(f'(c)\) does not exist.
Theorem 2.2 (Fermat's Theorem): If \(f\) has a local maximum or minimum at \(c\) and \(f'(c)\) exists, then \(f'(c) = 0\).
Proof: Suppose \(f\) has a local maximum at \(c\). Then for small \(h > 0\): - \(f(c+h) \leq f(c)\) implies \(\frac{f(c+h)-f(c)}{h} \leq 0\) - \(f(c-h) \leq f(c)\) implies \(\frac{f(c-h)-f(c)}{-h} \leq 0\) Taking limits: \(f'(c) = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{f(c+h)-f(c)}{h} \leq 0\) and \(f'(c) = \lim_{h \to 0^+} \frac{f(c-h)-f(c)}{h} \geq 0\) Therefore \(f'(c) = 0\). ∎

2.3 The Closed Interval Method

Closed Interval Method: To find the absolute maximum and minimum of \(f\) on \([a,b]\):
  1. Find all critical numbers of \(f\) in \((a,b)\)
  2. Evaluate \(f\) at each critical number and at the endpoints
  3. The largest value is the absolute maximum; the smallest is the absolute minimum
Example 2.1: Find the dimensions of the rectangle with perimeter 20 that has maximum area.

Let length = \(x\), width = \(y\). Constraint: \(2x + 2y = 20 \Rightarrow y = 10 - x\).

Area: \(A = xy = x(10-x) = 10x - x^2\)

Find critical point: \(A' = 10 - 2x = 0 \Rightarrow x = 5\)

Then \(y = 10 - 5 = 5\).

Maximum area: 25 square units (a square).

Example 2.2: Find the point on the parabola \(y = x^2\) closest to the point \((0, 1)\).

Distance squared: \(D = x^2 + (x^2 - 1)^2\)

Minimize: \(D' = 2x + 4x(x^2 - 1) = 2x(1 + 2x^2 - 2) = 2x(2x^2 - 1) = 0\)

Critical points: \(x = 0\) or \(x = \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\)

Minimum at \(x = \pm\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\), giving \(y = \frac{1}{2}\).

2.4 Optimization with Constraints (Lagrange Multipliers Intro)

Example 2.3: Find the dimensions of the largest right circular cylinder that can be inscribed in a sphere of radius \(R\).

Let cylinder have radius \(r\) and height \(h\). Constraint: \(r^2 + (h/2)^2 = R^2\).

Volume: \(V = \pi r^2 h\). Express \(h = 2\sqrt{R^2 - r^2}\).

Then \(V = 2\pi r^2\sqrt{R^2 - r^2}\).

Differentiate and set \(V' = 0\) to find optimal \(r = R\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\), \(h = \frac{2R}{\sqrt{3}}\).
