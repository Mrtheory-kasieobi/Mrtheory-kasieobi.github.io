Quadratic Equations

Precalculus 11 - Lecture Notes
British Columbia Curriculum | Last Updated: 2026

1. Introduction to Quadratic Equations

A quadratic equation is an equation of the form:

\[ax^2 + bx + c = 0\]

Where \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) are constants, and \(a \neq 0\).

Definition: A quadratic equation has at most two solutions, called roots or x-intercepts. These solutions can be real or complex numbers.

2. Graphical Solutions

Quadratic equations can be solved graphically by finding the x-intercepts of the parabola \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\).

2.1 Using Graphs to Solve

To solve \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\) graphically:

  1. Graph the function \(y = ax^2 + bx + c\)
  2. Identify the x-intercepts (where \(y = 0\))
  3. The x-coordinates of the intercepts are the solutions
Example 1: Graphical Solution

Solve \(x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0\) graphically.

Solution:

Graph \(y = x^2 - 5x + 6\)

The x-intercepts are at \(x = 2\) and \(x = 3\)

Solutions: \(x = 2\) or \(x = 3\)

3. Factoring Quadratic Equations

Factoring is one of the simplest methods for solving quadratic equations when the equation can be factored easily.

Zero Product Property:
If \(ab = 0\), then \(a = 0\) or \(b = 0\)

3.1 Factoring Methods

  • Common factoring: Factor out the GCF
  • Difference of squares: \(a^2 - b^2 = (a + b)(a - b)\)
  • Trinomial factoring: \(ax^2 + bx + c = (dx + e)(fx + g)\)
Example 2: Factoring a Trinomial

Solve \(x^2 - 5x + 6 = 0\) by factoring.

Solution:

Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to -5: -2 and -3

\[x^2 - 5x + 6 = (x - 2)(x - 3) = 0\]

Using the zero product property:

\(x - 2 = 0\) or \(x - 3 = 0\)

Solutions: \(x = 2\) or \(x = 3\)

3.2 Factoring with \(a \neq 1\)

When the leading coefficient is not 1, use the grouping method or the AC method:

Example 3: Factoring with \(a \neq 1\)

Solve \(2x^2 + 7x + 3 = 0\) by factoring.

Solution:

Multiply \(a \times c = 2 \times 3 = 6\)

Find two numbers that multiply to 6 and add to 7: 6 and 1

Split the middle term: \(2x^2 + 6x + x + 3 = 0\)

Factor by grouping: \(2x(x + 3) + 1(x + 3) = 0\)

\((2x + 1)(x + 3) = 0\)

Solutions: \(x = -\frac{1}{2}\) or \(x = -3\)

4. Completing the Square

Completing the square is a method that transforms a quadratic equation into vertex form, making it easy to solve.

4.1 Steps for Completing the Square

  1. Divide all terms by \(a\) (if \(a \neq 1\))
  2. Move the constant term to the right side
  3. Add \((\frac{b}{2})^2\) to both sides
  4. Factor the left side as a perfect square
  5. Solve for \(x\)
Example 4: Completing the Square

Solve \(x^2 + 6x + 4 = 0\) by completing the square.

Solution:

Step 1: Move constant: \(x^2 + 6x = -4\)

Step 2: Add \((\frac{6}{2})^2 = 9\) to both sides:

\[x^2 + 6x + 9 = -4 + 9\]

Step 3: Factor: \((x + 3)^2 = 5\)

Step 4: Take square root: \(x + 3 = \pm\sqrt{5}\)

Step 5: Solve: \(x = -3 \pm \sqrt{5}\)

Solutions: \(x \approx -5.24\) or \(x \approx -0.76\)

5. The Quadratic Formula

The quadratic formula provides a universal method for solving any quadratic equation:

\[x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\]

Where \(a\), \(b\), and \(c\) are the coefficients from \(ax^2 + bx + c = 0\).

5.1 The Discriminant

The expression under the square root, \(b^2 - 4ac\), is called the discriminant. It determines the nature of the roots:

\[D = b^2 - 4ac\]
  • If \(D > 0\): Two distinct real roots
  • If \(D = 0\): One real root (repeated)
  • If \(D < 0\): Two complex (non-real) roots
Example 5: Using the Quadratic Formula

Solve \(3x^2 + 2x - 1 = 0\) using the quadratic formula.

Solution:

\(a = 3\), \(b = 2\), \(c = -1\)

\[x = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{2^2 - 4(3)(-1)}}{2(3)}\]

\[x = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{4 + 12}}{6}\]

\[x = \frac{-2 \pm \sqrt{16}}{6}\]

\[x = \frac{-2 \pm 4}{6}\]

Solutions: \(x = \frac{2}{6} = \frac{1}{3}\) or \(x = \frac{-6}{6} = -1\)

6. Choosing a Solution Method

Method When to Use
Factoring When the quadratic factors easily
Completing the Square When \(a = 1\) and you need vertex form
Quadratic Formula Works for all quadratics, especially when factoring is difficult
Graphing To visualize solutions or when approximate answers are acceptable

7. Summary of Key Concepts

Concept Formula/Definition
Quadratic Formula \(x = \frac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}\)
Discriminant \(D = b^2 - 4ac\)
Zero Product Property If \(ab = 0\), then \(a = 0\) or \(b = 0\)
Completing the Square Add \((\frac{b}{2})^2\) to both sides
