A Rank-30 Elliptic Curve

August 21, 2026 ~5 min read Elliptic Curves

I first heard about this record in a YouTube video and then checked the details on the ICARM leaderboard. Entry #273 lists an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ with rank at least 30. The ICARM commentary credits the find to Claude, with Levent Alpöge and Ava Howell. The previous record was rank at least 29, set in 2024.

The curve is given by

$$y^2 + xy = x^3 + ax + b,$$

where $a = -201769035260418549083594900060734240952308696994802735114305555$ and $b = 1151107939141058565733479426024323225135665982951300586808823640527729578307228357301072889377$. The large coefficients are typical of current high-rank records.

Elliptic curves and rank

The short Weierstrass form $y^2 = x^3 + ax + b$ is more familiar, but $y^2 + xy = x^3 + ax + b$ is also a Weierstrass equation. It defines an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ provided its discriminant $\Delta$ is nonzero.

The set of rational points $E(\mathbb{Q})$ forms an abelian group under the chord-and-tangent law. By the Mordell–Weil theorem,

$$E(\mathbb{Q}) \cong E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}} \oplus \mathbb{Z}^r,$$

where $E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ is the finite torsion subgroup and $r \in \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ is the Mordell–Weil rank. Thus $r$ is the rank of the free abelian part of $E(\mathbb{Q})$.

What "rank at least 30" means

The claim is a lower bound. To prove $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q}) \ge 30$, it suffices to exhibit 30 rational points $P_1,\ldots,P_{30} \in E(\mathbb{Q})$ whose images in $E(\mathbb{Q})/E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ are $\mathbb{Z}$-linearly independent. Equivalently, if

$$n_1 P_1 + n_2 P_2 + \cdots + n_{30} P_{30} \in E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$$

for integers $n_1,\ldots,n_{30}$, then $n_1=\cdots=n_{30}=0$. Since Curve #273 has trivial torsion, this reduces to the relation $n_1P_1+\cdots+n_{30}P_{30}=\mathcal{O}$.

This is weaker than determining the exact rank. Independent points establish a lower bound; proving an upper bound requires separate arguments, such as descent or analytic rank bounds.

Why the record is notable

The 30 witness points have large coordinates, and their independence has been certified computationally. ICARM requires such certificates for leaderboard entries.

Curve #273 also holds other records among known rank-30 curves, including smallest discriminant.

Record progression

  • 2006: Elkies finds a curve with rank $\ge 28$
  • 2024: Elkies and Klagsbrun reach rank $\ge 29$
  • August 20, 2026: Alpöge, Howell, and Claude submit Curve #273 with rank $\ge 30$

The rank-28 record stood for 18 years. The recent increases reflect progress in search methods and verification, but each step still requires substantial computation.

Conditional exact rank

Under the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, ICARM certifies the rank as exactly 30. This is conditional on BSD and GRH, not an unconditional determination of the rank.

Update - August 23, 2026

A new curve with rank at least 31 was reported shortly after this post. See the follow-up post on the rank-31 curve.

Open question

It is still unknown whether ranks of elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ are bounded. A rank-30 curve raises the known lower bound; it does not settle whether arbitrarily large ranks occur.
