A Rank-31 Elliptic Curve

August 23, 2026 ~8 min read Elliptic Curves

The ICARM leaderboard lists Curve #302 with a certified lower bound of $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q}) \ge 31$. The curve was submitted on August 23, 2026, three days after the rank-30 record.

The recent progression is rank at least 28 in 2006, 29 in 2024, 30 on August 20, 2026, and 31 on August 23, 2026.

Background: rank

An elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ can be written in Weierstrass form

$$y^2 + a_1xy + a_3y = x^3 + a_2x^2 + a_4x + a_6,$$

with nonzero discriminant. The rational points $E(\mathbb{Q})$ form an abelian group under the chord-and-tangent law. By the Mordell–Weil theorem,

$$E(\mathbb{Q}) \cong E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}} \oplus \mathbb{Z}^r,$$

where $E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ is finite and $r \ge 0$ is the Mordell–Weil rank.

The 31 witness points

The lower bound is established by exhibiting 31 rational points $P_1,\ldots,P_{31}\in E(\mathbb{Q})$ whose images in $E(\mathbb{Q})/E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}}$ are $\mathbb{Z}$-linearly independent. Since the torsion subgroup is trivial, this is equivalent to saying that no nontrivial integer relation $\sum_{i=1}^{31} n_iP_i=\mathcal{O}$ holds. ICARM lists these witness points and provides a computational independence certificate.

What has been established

  • Unconditionally: the 31 exhibited rational points are independent, so $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q}) \geq 31$.
  • Conditionally, under BSD and GRH: ICARM certifies the rank as exactly 31.

The lower bound does not depend on BSD or GRH. The exact-rank statement does.

Record progression

  • 2006: Noam Elkies finds rank $\geq 28$
  • 2024: Elkies and Zev Klagsbrun reach rank $\geq 29$
  • August 20, 2026: Curve #273 reaches rank $\geq 30$
  • August 23, 2026: Curve #302 reaches rank $\geq 31$

Curve #273 and Curve #302 are different curves, with different equations and different witness points.

Why this matters

High-rank examples are difficult to find because the curve coefficients, point coordinates, and independence checks become large. The computation must produce both the points and a certificate of their independence.

The main open question remains unchanged: are ranks of elliptic curves over $\mathbb{Q}$ bounded? Curve #302 raises the known lower bound to 31.
