A Rank-31 Elliptic Curve: A New Record in the Search for High-Rank Curves

August 23, 2026 ~8 min read Elliptic Curves

The ICARM Elliptic Curve Rank Leaderboard now lists Curve #302 with a certified rank lower bound of at least 31, establishing a new record for the largest currently known Mordell-Weil rank lower bound for an elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$. This supersedes the rank-30 record established just three days earlier by ICARM Curve #273.

The known rank lower bound has increased from 28 (in 2006) to 29 (in 2024), to 30 (August 20, 2026), and now to 31 (August 23, 2026)-an exceptionally rapid progression in high-rank elliptic curve research.

What Does the Rank of an Elliptic Curve Mean?

An elliptic curve over $\mathbb{Q}$ can be written in general Weierstrass form:

$$y^2 + a_1xy + a_3y = x^3 + a_2x^2 + a_4x + a_6$$

The set of rational points $E(\mathbb{Q})$ naturally forms an abelian group under the chord-and-tangent construction. The Mordell–Weil theorem gives the canonical decomposition:

$$E(\mathbb{Q}) \cong E(\mathbb{Q})_{\mathrm{tors}} \oplus \mathbb{Z}^r$$

The integer $r$ is the Mordell-Weil rank. It measures the number of independent generators of the free part of the rational points on the curve.

Thirty-One Independent Rational Points

ICARM provides 31 witness points $P_1, P_2, \ldots, P_{31}$ as part of the certification that the curve has rank at least 31. The points are used as witnesses for the rank lower bound: exhibiting 31 independent rational points proves that the Mordell-Weil rank is at least 31.

What Exactly Has Been Established?

It is important to distinguish two statements:

  • Unconditionally: The exhibited 31 independent rational points establish that $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q}) \geq 31$.
  • Conditionally (under BSD + GRH): ICARM marks the curve as "BSD + GRH certified to rank 31," meaning that under the Birch and Swinnerton-Dyer conjecture and the Generalized Riemann Hypothesis, the exact rank is 31.

The lower-bound result is unconditional. The exact-rank conclusion depends on the BSD conjecture and GRH.

From Rank 28 to Rank 31

The progression of high-rank elliptic curve records:

  • 2006: Noam Elkies established rank $\geq 28$ with curve 2.a8
  • 2024: Noam Elkies and Zev Klagsbrun pushed to rank $\geq 29$
  • August 20, 2026: ICARM Curve #273 achieved rank $\geq 30$
  • August 23, 2026: ICARM Curve #302 established rank $\geq 31$

Curve #273 and Curve #302 are distinct curves with different equations and different sets of witness points.

Why Is Rank 31 Significant?

Elliptic curve ranks are difficult to make large. Finding 31 independent rational points on a single curve requires:

  • A curve with enormous coefficients (this curve has coefficients with ~40 digits)
  • Verifying independence of points with enormous coordinates
  • Certifying within ICARM's computational framework

The open question in number theory is whether there exists an absolute bound $B$ such that every elliptic curve $E/\mathbb{Q}$ satisfies $\operatorname{rank} E(\mathbb{Q}) \leq B$. Finding rank-31 curves does not answer this question-it merely raises the known lower bound.

The Unusual Role of Claude

The ICARM record attributes the discovery to "Claude, Levent Alpöge, and Ava Howell." This provides an interesting example of AI-assisted or AI-involved computational mathematics. Detailed conclusions about the precise division of computational work between the AI system and human researchers should await a primary account of the discovery process. The rapid succession of rank records-from 28 in 2006 to 31 in August 2026-represents an unusual acceleration in this computational problem.

What Comes Next?

A rank-32 elliptic curve would establish a new record and provide further data about the distribution of ranks. Computational searches continue to explore coefficient spaces for curves that might yield higher ranks. Information about Curve #302 beyond what ICARM currently provides, including the complete point coordinates, may become available through future updates or publications from the discoverers.
