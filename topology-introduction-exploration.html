A Brief Look at Topology: Open, Closed, and Clopen Sets

July 19, 2026 ~4 min read Learning Journal

Although I am not currently studying topology in depth, I recently became curious about some of its foundational ideas after encountering references to it in other areas of mathematics. I decided to learn the basic definitions to get a sense of what the subject is about. This short note summarizes my first impressions of topological spaces and the notions of open, closed, and clopen sets.

Basic Definitions

A topology on a set $X$ is a collection $\mathcal{T}$ of subsets of $X$ satisfying three conditions:

  • Both the empty set $\varnothing$ and the entire set $X$ belong to $\mathcal{T}$.
  • The union of any collection of sets in $\mathcal{T}$ is again an element of $\mathcal{T}$.
  • The intersection of finitely many sets in $\mathcal{T}$ is also an element of $\mathcal{T}$.

The pair $(X,\mathcal{T})$ is called a topological space, and the members of $\mathcal{T}$ are called open sets.

Open and Closed Sets

Unlike Euclidean geometry, topology does not begin with distances or angles. Instead, it specifies which subsets should be regarded as "open," and from this surprisingly small amount of information many geometric and analytical concepts can be developed.

A subset $A \subseteq X$ is called closed if its complement $X \setminus A$ is open. Consequently, openness and closedness are dual notions: every open set determines a closed set, and vice versa. A set need not be exclusively open or closed, it may be one, the other, both, or neither.

Clopen Sets

The empty set and the entire space are always both open and closed. Sets with this property are called clopen. In many familiar topological spaces these are the only clopen sets, but in more general settings nontrivial clopen sets are closely connected to the notion of connectedness.

Example

As a simple example, let

$$X = \{a,b,c\}, \quad \mathcal{T} = \{\varnothing, \{a\}, \{a,b\}, X\}$$

One can verify that $\mathcal{T}$ satisfies the topology axioms. The open sets are precisely the elements of $\mathcal{T}$, while the closed sets are their complements:

  • Open sets: $\{\varnothing, \{a\}, \{a,b\}, X\}$
  • Closed sets: $\{X, \{b,c\}, \{c\}, \varnothing\}$

Thus, $\varnothing$ and $X$ are clopen, $\{a\}$ is open but not closed, and $\{c\}$ is closed but not open.

Reflection

This brief exploration gave me an appreciation for how much mathematical structure can arise from a small collection of axioms. While topology is not one of the subjects I am actively studying at the moment, I found these foundational definitions interesting enough to learn and document. I may revisit the subject in the future once I have a stronger background in areas such as real analysis and abstract algebra.
