Rational Expressions and Equations

Precalculus 11 - Lecture Notes
British Columbia Curriculum | Last Updated: 2026

1. Introduction to Rational Expressions

A rational expression is a fraction in which the numerator and/or denominator are polynomials.

Definition: A rational expression is of the form \(\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\), where \(P(x)\) and \(Q(x)\) are polynomials and \(Q(x) \neq 0\).

1.1 Domain of Rational Expressions

The domain consists of all real numbers except those that make the denominator equal to zero.

Example 1: Finding the Domain

Find the domain of \(\frac{x + 2}{x^2 - 9}\)

Solution:

Set denominator equal to zero: \(x^2 - 9 = 0\)

\(x^2 = 9\)

\(x = \pm 3\)

Domain: All real numbers except \(x \neq \pm 3\)

2. Simplifying Rational Expressions

To simplify a rational expression, factor the numerator and denominator, then cancel common factors.

Steps to Simplify:
1. Factor numerator and denominator completely
2. Cancel common factors
3. State restrictions (values that make the original denominator zero)
Example 2: Simplifying Rational Expressions

Simplify: \(\frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - 6x + 9}\)

Solution:

Factor numerator: \(x^2 - 9 = (x + 3)(x - 3)\)

Factor denominator: \(x^2 - 6x + 9 = (x - 3)^2\)

\[\frac{x^2 - 9}{x^2 - 6x + 9} = \frac{(x + 3)(x - 3)}{(x - 3)(x - 3)} = \frac{x + 3}{x - 3}\]

Restriction: \(x \neq 3\)

3. Multiplying and Dividing Rational Expressions

3.1 Multiplying Rational Expressions

To multiply rational expressions, multiply the numerators and denominators, then simplify:

\[\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \cdot \frac{R(x)}{S(x)} = \frac{P(x) \cdot R(x)}{Q(x) \cdot S(x)}\]
Example 3: Multiplying Rational Expressions

Multiply: \(\frac{x^2 - 4}{x^2 - x - 6} \cdot \frac{x + 2}{x - 2}\)

Solution:

Factor: \(\frac{(x + 2)(x - 2)}{(x - 3)(x + 2)} \cdot \frac{x + 2}{x - 2}\)

Multiply: \(\frac{(x + 2)(x - 2)(x + 2)}{(x - 3)(x + 2)(x - 2)}\)

Cancel: \(\frac{x + 2}{x - 3}\)

Restrictions: \(x \neq 3, x \neq -2, x \neq 2\)

3.2 Dividing Rational Expressions

To divide rational expressions, multiply by the reciprocal of the divisor:

\[\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \div \frac{R(x)}{S(x)} = \frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \cdot \frac{S(x)}{R(x)}\]
Example 4: Dividing Rational Expressions

Divide: \(\frac{x^2 - 1}{x^2 + 5x + 6} \div \frac{x - 1}{x + 3}\)

Solution:

Multiply by reciprocal: \(\frac{x^2 - 1}{x^2 + 5x + 6} \cdot \frac{x + 3}{x - 1}\)

Factor: \(\frac{(x + 1)(x - 1)}{(x + 3)(x + 2)} \cdot \frac{x + 3}{x - 1}\)

Cancel: \(\frac{x + 1}{x + 2}\)

Restrictions: \(x \neq -3, x \neq -2, x \neq 1\)

4. Adding and Subtracting Rational Expressions

To add or subtract rational expressions, find a common denominator, then combine the numerators.

4.1 Finding the Least Common Denominator (LCD)

The LCD is the least common multiple of the denominators.

Example 5: Adding Rational Expressions

Add: \(\frac{2}{x - 1} + \frac{3}{x + 2}\)

Solution:

LCD = \((x - 1)(x + 2)\)

\[\frac{2(x + 2)}{(x - 1)(x + 2)} + \frac{3(x - 1)}{(x + 2)(x - 1)}\]

\[= \frac{2x + 4 + 3x - 3}{(x - 1)(x + 2)}\]

\[= \frac{5x + 1}{(x - 1)(x + 2)}\]

Restrictions: \(x \neq 1, x \neq -2\)

4.2 Adding with Like Denominators

When denominators are the same, simply add or subtract the numerators:

\[\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)} \pm \frac{R(x)}{Q(x)} = \frac{P(x) \pm R(x)}{Q(x)}\]

5. Rational Equations

A rational equation is an equation containing rational expressions.

Definition: A rational equation is an equation that involves at least one rational expression.

5.1 Solving Rational Equations

To solve a rational equation:

  1. Find the LCD of all denominators
  2. Multiply every term by the LCD
  3. Solve the resulting polynomial equation
  4. Check for extraneous solutions
Example 6: Solving a Rational Equation

Solve: \(\frac{2}{x} + \frac{3}{x - 1} = 1\)

Solution:

LCD = \(x(x - 1)\)

Multiply by LCD: \(2(x - 1) + 3x = x(x - 1)\)

\(2x - 2 + 3x = x^2 - x\)

\(5x - 2 = x^2 - x\)

\(x^2 - 6x + 2 = 0\)

Using quadratic formula: \(x = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{36 - 8}}{2} = \frac{6 \pm \sqrt{28}}{2}\)

Check: Neither solution makes any denominator zero

5.2 Extraneous Solutions

When solving rational equations, always check solutions in the original equation. Solutions that make any denominator zero are extraneous and must be rejected.

Important:

Always state the restrictions before solving, and check all solutions in the original equation to avoid extraneous solutions.

6. Summary of Key Concepts

Concept Formula/Definition
Rational Expression \(\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}\) where \(Q(x) \neq 0\)
Multiplying \(\frac{P}{Q} \cdot \frac{R}{S} = \frac{PR}{QS}\)
Dividing \(\frac{P}{Q} \div \frac{R}{S} = \frac{P}{Q} \cdot \frac{S}{R}\)
Adding/Subtracting Find LCD, then combine numerators
Solving Rational Equations Multiply by LCD, solve, check for extraneous solutions

7. Practice Problems

Problem 1

Problem 2

Problem 3
